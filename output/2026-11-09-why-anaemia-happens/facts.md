# Why does anaemia (రక్తహీనత) happen? (1.30 min, Health Decode)

- Anaemia = too few red blood cells or too little haemoglobin, so blood carries less oxygen to the body — WHO fact sheet (Feb 2025) https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/anaemia — high
- Haemoglobin (in red blood cells) is what carries oxygen — WHO — high
- Most common nutritional cause: iron deficiency; folate, vitamin B12 and vitamin A deficiencies also important — WHO — high
- Symptoms: tiredness, weakness, dizziness/light-headedness, shortness of breath especially on exertion; also pale skin, headaches, palpitations — WHO; NHS https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/iron-deficiency-anaemia/ — high (non-specific; don't confirm anaemia alone)
- India NFHS-5 (2019–21): 57% of women aged 15–49 anaemic (up from 53.1%); 67.1% of children 6–59 months (up from 58.6%) — Down To Earth https://www.downtoearth.org.in/health/anaemia-in-women-and-children-on-the-rise-assam-fares-worst-nfhs-5-80357 — high
- Global (WHO, 2019): ~30% of non-pregnant women 15–49 — WHO — high
- Diagnosis: a blood test (full blood count) checks red blood cell / haemoglobin levels — NHS — high
- Iron-rich foods: dark-green leafy vegetables, pulses (beans, peas, lentils), meat, dried fruit, fortified cereals — NHS — high
- Iron tablets: only on doctor's advice; overdose dangerous for children — NHS — high

## Angle
- Hook with the India stat (more than half of women).
- Normal first: red blood cells + haemoglobin = oxygen delivery trucks; iron is what haemoglobin is built with.
- What goes wrong: not enough iron/vitamins → less haemoglobin → less oxygen → tiredness, breathlessness.
- Misconception: tiredness is often brushed off as "just weakness".
- Safety: symptoms are general → get a blood test, take tablets only on doctor's advice.
