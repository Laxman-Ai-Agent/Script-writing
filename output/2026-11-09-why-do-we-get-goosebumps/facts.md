# Why do we get goosebumps? (2 min)

- Each hair follicle has a tiny muscle, the arrector pili; when it contracts the hair stands up and the skin around it bumps up — Live Science https://www.livescience.com/32349-what-causes-goose-bumps.html — high
- Triggered by the sympathetic nervous system (the body's fight-or-flight system); nerves wrapped around the muscle signal it to contract → why cold, fear and strong emotion all cause it — Live Science; NIH Research Matters https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/what-goosebumps-are — high
- In furry mammals, raised hair traps a layer of air for insulation, or makes the animal look bigger to a threat (e.g. a frightened cat) — Live Science; Salon 2020 https://salon.com/2020/07/22/goosebumps-may-be-a-side-effect-of-hair-follicle-muscles-scientists-say — high
- In humans, with little body hair, goosebumps give almost no warmth benefit; often described as a leftover from mammalian ancestors — Live Science (expert: "relic") — high
- 2020 Cell paper (Shwartz et al., Ya-Chieh Hsu, Harvard + National Taiwan University), in MICE: the same sympathetic nerve also wraps around hair follicle stem cells; the muscle acts as a bridge; short term cold → goosebumps, longer cold → stem cell activation and new hair growth; removing the muscle delayed new hair growth — Harvard HSCRB https://hscrb.harvard.edu/publication/shwartz-y-gonzalez-celeiro-m-chen-cl-pasolli-ha-sheu-sh-fan-sm-shamsi-f-assaad-s-lin-et-zhang-b-tsai-pc-he-m-tseng-yh-lin-sj-hsu-yc-cell-types-promoting-goosebumps-form-a-niche-to-regul ; News-Medical https://www.news-medical.net/news/20200720/Harvard-scientists-uncover-the-real-reason-behind-goosebumps.aspx — high (mouse study)
- Human relevance / baldness treatment — speculative → mention only as "మనుషుల్లో ఇంకా తేలాల్సి ఉంది"

## Angle
- Everyday hook: cold wind, a scary scene, a favourite song — all give the same reaction.
- Core: a tiny muscle per hair + the fight-or-flight nerve system.
- Twist 1: it made sense for furry ancestors (cat puffing up).
- Twist 2: maybe not just a leftover — 2020 mouse study links it to hair growth.
- Misconception: "goosebumps are useless" — partly true for warmth, but the system may still do another job.
