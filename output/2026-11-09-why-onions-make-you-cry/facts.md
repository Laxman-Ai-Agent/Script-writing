# Why do onions make you cry? (1.30 min)

- Irritant is not stored in the onion; it forms only when cells are cut/crushed — The Conversation (Mellor, 2017) https://theconversation.com/why-onions-make-us-cry-and-why-some-dont-84486 — high
- Chain: alliinase acts on sulphur compound (PRENCSO 1) → 1-propenyl sulphenic acid; a second enzyme, lachrymatory factor synthase (LFS), turns it into syn-propanethial-S-oxide — The Conversation; PubMed 28708375 (LFS crystal structure, 2017) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28708375/ — high
- The compound is volatile → evaporates and drifts to the eyes — The Conversation; phys.org 2017 https://phys.org/news/2017-08-nitty-gritty-onions.amp — high
- Cornea has dense sensory nerve endings (ciliary branch of trigeminal nerve); they detect it and reflexively trigger lacrimal glands to wash it away (reflex tears) — Scientific American https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/what-is-the-chemical-proc/ — high
- Amount is irritating, not harmful — The Conversation — high
- Purpose: defence against pests/animals — The Conversation — high (accepted explanation)
- Cornell study (Wu et al., PNAS, 2025): high-speed video of blade cutting onion; droplets ejected at 5–40 m/s; blunter blades and faster cuts → more droplets; recommendation: sharp knife, cut slowly — physicsworld https://physicsworld.com/a/the-physics-behind-why-cutting-onions-makes-us-cry/ ; Science News https://www.sciencenews.org/article/knife-tricks-stop-onion-cutting-crying — high
- Chilling: older advice says chill to slow reaction; Cornell found chilled onions gave MORE droplets (authors say needs more study) — disputed → omit from script
- TRPA1 receptor — single source (Spektrum) → omit

## Angle
- Twist: the tear gas doesn't exist until the knife cuts — the onion "builds a weapon" in that instant.
- Misconception: people think onion juice splashes into the eye; actually a gas (plus fine droplets) drifts up.
- Modern hook: 2025 high-speed camera study → sharp knife + slow cut.
