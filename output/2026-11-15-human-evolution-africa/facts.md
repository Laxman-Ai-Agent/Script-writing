# Why humans evolved in Africa

Note: sources were checked via search-result summaries (not every page fetched in full). Items marked "verify" need a primary-paper check before final.

## Origins and climate
- Humans did NOT evolve from chimps/monkeys; we share a common ancestor with chimps/bonobos, split roughly 6-7 Mya (molecular + fossil estimates vary) — https://humanorigins.si.edu — medium (exact split date varies by source; verify)
- East Africa: Rift formation + long-term drying trend; Maslin and Trauth: drying was interrupted by short pulses of extreme wet/dry (precession-driven), Rift lakes appear and vanish; pulses tied to hominin events e.g. speciation/brain expansion ~1.8 Mya — https://pure.royalholloway.ac.uk/en/publications/east-african-climate-pulses-and-early-human-evolution/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4305165 — medium (authors themselves say mechanisms still debated)
- "East Side Story" (Yves Coppens): Rift Valley as a barrier; east of it dries to savanna, ancestors there go bipedal, west side apes stay in forest — https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Savannah_hypothesis — high that this is the idea; disputed as true
- Open-grassland savanna version is mostly dismissed; mosaic habitat (woodland + grass) version has wider support. Ardipithecus (4.4 Mya) found in woodland-ish setting (2009); Cerling et al. 2010 argued it was more savanna-like (5-25% tree cover); White's team disagreed — https://www.sciencenews.org/article/contested-evidence-pushes-ardi-out-woods — disputed
- Sahelanthropus tchadensis ("Toumai"), Chad, ~7 Mya, skull found 2001 by Michel Brunet's team; foramen magnum placement suggested upright posture. Chad is ~2,500 km west of the Rift, which undercut East Side Story (distance figure: verify) — https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/new-fossil-analysis-suggests-this-seven-million-year-old-primate-walked-on-two-legs-potentially-making-it-the-oldest-known-human-ancestor-180987947/ — medium
- Sahelanthropus bipedalism still debated: 2022 Daver/Guy (Nature) said habitual biped from femur; 2025 rebuttal (Cazenave) disputed; Jan 2026 Science Advances (Williams et al.) reports femoral tubercle + torsion, critics call evidence weak/bone too damaged — https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/jan/04/when-did-humanity-take-its-first-step-scientists-s/ ; https://news.cnrs.fr/print/2121 — disputed
- Bipedal energy: Sockol, Raichlen, Pontzer 2007 (PNAS 104:12265). Human walking ~75% cheaper than chimp locomotion (either gait); chimp bipedal vs knuckle-walking only ~10% costlier, not significant when pooled; individual chimps differed, longer-strided chimp did bipedal best. So "bipedal saves energy vs knuckle-walking" is a human-vs-chimp claim, NOT chimp upright being cheaper — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1941460 ; https://www.nature.com/news/2007/070716/full/news070716-2.html — high on numbers (as reported in summaries)
- Variability selection (Rick Potts): fluctuating environments favoured adaptable traits/behaviours; Olorgesailie, Kenya, evidence; high climate variability ~275-180 ka coincides with first H. sapiens in East Africa — https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/article/adaptable-human ; https://global.si.edu/success-stories/investigating-how-shifting-climate-shaped-human-evolution — medium (hypothesis, not proven)
- Why Africa: ape lineage there (common ancestor), Rift basins trap sediment/volcanic ash so fossils preserve and date well (argon-argon on ash layers) — Rift lake record: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4305165 — medium (preservation point is general knowledge; bias caveat: Africa also best-searched)

## Key fossils
- Laetoli footprints, Tanzania, ~3.66 Mya, attributed to Australopithecus afarensis; found 1978; 2016 eLife: new tracks, one individual ~1.65 m tall -> size variation — https://elifesciences.org/content/5/e19568 — high
- Lucy, A. afarensis, Hadar Ethiopia, found 1974, ~3.2 Mya (3.18 in some sources); ~40% skeleton (percent: verify) — https://popular-archaeology.com/?p=5628 ; https://www.johnhawks.net/p/new-footprints-from-laetoli — high on date/place
- Lomekwi 3, West Turkana Kenya, stone tools 3.3 Mya (Nature 2015, Harmand et al.), maker unknown (maybe Kenyanthropus/afarensis); dated via ash layer — https://www.sci.news/archaeology/science-lomekwi-3-oldest-stone-tools-02822.html ; https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2015/05/150520131659.htm — high
- Oldowan ~2.6 Mya (Ethiopia, Gona/Ledi-Geraru) — mentioned as "2.6 Mya Ethiopia" in Lomekwi coverage above — high (Ledi-Geraru specifics not separately confirmed)
- Swartkrans SK 54: juvenile Paranthropus robustus cranium (found 1949); two punctures 33 mm apart fit leopard lower canines (C.K. Brain, 1960s-70s); age cited 1.5-2 Mya depending on source. Species is P. robustus, NOT our ancestor genus Homo — https://australian.museum/learn/science/human-evolution/how-do-we-know-how-they-died/ ; https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/have-the-hunting-habits-of-leopards-shaped-primate-evolution — high
- Paranthropus: P. boisei East Africa ~2.3-1.2 Mya; P. robustus South Africa; genus gone by ~1 Mya (extinction ~1.2-1 Mya) — https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/738754 — medium (exact last dates vary; "1 Mya" is roundish)
- P. boisei diet: ~75-80% C4 plants (grasses/sedges), more than any hominin studied (PNAS 2011) -> NOT "nutcracker man" — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3111323 — high
- P. robustus diet (Science 2006): varied (fruit, nuts, sedges, grasses, seeds, maybe animals); authors doubted diet specialization caused extinction. So "couldn't change diet" claim is shaky — https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2006/11/061109155649.htm — medium/disputed

## Homo, fire, leaving Africa
- Homo erectus at Dmanisi, Georgia, 1.85-1.77 Mya — https://blogs.scientificamerican.com/anthropology-in-practice/hominins-likely-left-africa-earlier-than-believed/ — high
- Shangchen, China (Loess Plateau): stone tools ~2.1 Mya (Nature 2018, Zhu et al.), 96 artifacts, NO fossils so toolmaker unknown; dating questioned (Ferring: reversal may be ~1.95 Mya) — https://www.sciencenews.org/article/shangchen-stone-tools-put-early-hominids-china-earlier ; https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/news-china-human-tools-africa-shangchen-hominin-paleoanthropology — medium/disputed
- Fire: Wonderwerk Cave (South Africa) evidence ~1 Mya of burnt material (re-examination suggests maybe older ~1.8 Mya, unconfirmed); Gesher Benot Ya'aqov, Israel hearths ~780-800 ka (strongest early habitual-use case); other sources say clear habitual fire ~400 ka and later; Qesem ~300 ka — https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1124251 ; https://www.discovermagazine.com/charcoal-at-an-800-000-year-old-campsite-suggests-early-humans-were-drawn-to-an-abundant-supply-of-driftwood-48995 — disputed (say "earliest hints ~1 Mya, regular use hundreds of thousands of years later")
- Homo sapiens origin: Jebel Irhoud, Morocco, ~300 ka (315 +/- 34 ka thermoluminescence), Nature 2017 (Hublin; Richter); mosaic: modern face, primitive braincase; "pan-African origin" — not one Eden. Other early: Florisbad ~260 ka, Omo Kibish ~195 ka — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28593953/ ; https://www.eva.mpg.de/homo-sapiens.html ; https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/06/170607133241.htm — high
- Early failed exits: Misliya, Israel maxilla 177-194 ka (Science 2018); Apidima 1, Greece skull ~210 ka (Nature 2019, Harvati; date uncertain, maybe ~195 ka) — both left no lasting population — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29371468/ ; https://www.livescience.com/65906-oldest-modern-human-skull-eurasia.html — medium
- Main dispersal: all non-Africans descend mostly from one exit ~70-50 ka (various estimates 40-80 ka) — 2016 Nature genome trio; 2025 Science Advances (Gandini) says single dispersal supported, southern coastal route possible but contentious — https://www.the-scientist.com/out-of-africa-theory-gets-the-genomic-treatment-32807 ; https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/507195/3/sciadv.ady9493.pdf — medium (routes: Bab-el-Mandeb vs Sinai not confirmed; say "debated")
- Why leave when they did: Hallett et al. 2025 Nature — humans widened their habitat range inside Africa (forest to desert) BEFORE leaving, not an escape; ~70-50 ka the exit route was harder than earlier wet windows. Earlier exits tracked the Saharo-Arabian desert shrinking (Green Sahara windows) — https://www.mpg.de/24882137/before-dispersing-out-of-africa-humans-learned-to-thrive-in-diverse-habitats ; https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/06/250625111542.htm — high (2 sources)
- Green Sahara: African Humid Period ~11,000-5,000 ya (orbital wobble -> stronger monsoon); Cave of Swimmers, Wadi Sura, Gilf Kebir, Egypt: rock art ~9,000-6,000 ya, found by Almasy 1933 (swimmer interpretation debated); satellite radar (Skonieczny et al.) revealed buried river channel >300 miles in W. Sahara, last flowed ~6,500 ya — https://www.earthdate.org/episodes/swimming-in-the-sahara ; https://www.scientificamerican.com/podcast/episode/sahara-reveals-remains-of-ancient-river/ — medium. NOTE: AHP is far younger than human exit; but earlier green phases opened Sahara routes (~100k-year cycles), per Apidima coverage.
- Australia: Madjedbebe ~65 ka (Nature 2017, optical dating); some dispute — https://www.nature.com/nature-index/article/10.1038/nature22968 ; https://www.washington.edu/news/2017/07/19/artifacts-suggest-humans-arrived-in-australia-earlier-than-thought/ — medium
- Americas: White Sands footprints 21,000-23,000 ya (Science 2021; confirmed 2023 with pollen + quartz dating) — https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/fossil-footprints-challenge-theory-when-people-first-arrived-americas ; https://www.knau.org/knau-and-arizona-news/2023-10-06/further-evidence-points-to-footprints-in-new-mexico-being-the-oldest-sign-of-humans-in-americas — high (2 sources). "15-20k" in competitor video is outdated.

## Genes
- Africa has highest human genetic diversity, largest long-term population, deepest lineage splits; non-African variation is largely a subset (serial founder effect, diversity falls with distance from Africa) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3497766 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2947357/ — high
- Neanderthal DNA: ~2% in non-Africans; 2020 Cell: Africans also ~0.5% (via back-migration). Denisovan DNA ~4-6% in Melanesians/Pacific — https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/new-research-expands-neanderthals-genetic-legacy-modern-humans-180974099/ ; https://discovery.princeton.edu/2019/12/09/origin-story-rewriting-human-history-through-our-dna/ — high on 2%, medium on Denisovan 4-6% (single source; verify)
- Mitochondrial Eve: most recent common ancestor via mother-to-daughter line only; estimates ~100-230 ka (often ~150-200 ka); NOT the only woman alive then, not first woman; many contemporaries are our ancestors via other lines — https://www.bionity.com/en/encyclopedia/Mt-mrca.html — medium (secondary sources only)

## Surprising numbers
- Human walking costs ~75% LESS energy than chimp walking (both gaits)
- Toumai (7 Mya) was found ~2,500 km from the Rift where theory said it should be
- Stone tools (3.3 Mya) are older than the genus Homo (~2.8 Mya, not sourced; verify) and ~700,000 years older than Oldowan
- Leopard holes in a child's skull are 33 mm apart
- Non-Africans: ~2% Neanderthal DNA, yet more genetic variety lives in Africa than in the rest of the world combined (framing: verify as strict claim; safe version = "most of Africa's variation is not found elsewhere")

## Angle
- Core twist: we did not "leave a home" so much as Africa made us adaptable. Climate swung between wet and dry in pulses, so the winners were flexible generalists (tools, varied food, fire, groups). Leaving (~60k) was the result of that flexibility, not an escape from failure. And the tragedy of rigid specialists: Paranthropus.
- Second twist: no single Garden of Eden: Homo sapiens emerged across Africa (~300k, Jebel Irhoud to South Africa), and only one small exit ~70-50k seeded all non-Africans, after earlier exits failed.
- Misconceptions to correct from the competitor video:
  1. "Rift made forests vanish, so apes came down" — too neat; current view: mosaic habitats, Ardipithecus lived in woodland, Rift timing/location debated (Sahelanthropus in Chad). Present as a hypothesis.
  2. "Bipedal walking saves energy vs knuckle-walking" — in chimps the saving is not clear (~10% extra cost, not significant); the big saving is human walking vs chimps (~75% cheaper). Also energy was one proposed reason, not proven.
  3. "Chad skull proves bipedalism at 7 Mya" — still debated; foramen magnum position only a clue, femur analyses contested (2022 vs 2025 vs 2026).
  4. "Paranthropus died because they couldn't change diet" — boisei ate mostly C4 grasses/sedges; robustus had varied diet; cause of extinction unresolved. Dates ~1.2-1 Mya.
  5. "Orangutans never changed" — orangutans are Asian great apes that also evolved; staying in trees is a different branch, not a frozen ancestor. (Not source-checked; general biology.)
  6. "Child skull with leopard holes" — it's Paranthropus robustus (SK 54), not an ancestor of ours; shows early hominins were prey.
  7. "Fire" — earliest hints ~1 Mya (maybe older), strongest early evidence ~800 ka Israel, habitual use debated; don't say a single date.
  8. "Erectus left Africa ~1.8 Mya" — Dmanisi 1.8 Mya has fossils; Shangchen tools 2.1 Mya have no fossils and a disputed date.
  9. "Sahara green so humans walked out" — the humid period in cave art (~9-6 ka) is much younger than exit; earlier green windows mattered for earlier exits.
  10. "Leaving ~60-70k; Americas 15-20k" — Americas now ~21-23k (White Sands); Australia ~65k; earlier failed exits ~180-210k.
  11. Humans did not descend from chimps; shared ancestor ~6-7 Mya.
