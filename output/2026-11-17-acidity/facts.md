# Acidity (acid reflux / GERD)

## Why it matters (numbers)
- Global GERD prevalence ~14% (95% CI 12.5-15.6), about 1.03 billion people; varies by region/definition — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7118109 — high
- Weekly heartburn/regurgitation ~13.3% worldwide (Eusebi, Gut 2018) — https://gut.bmj.com/content/gutjnl/67/3/430.full.pdf — high
- India pooled prevalence ~15.6% (95% CI 11.0-20.7; 9 studies, 20,614 people; range 5-28.5%); full text says 14.15% — heterogeneity very high — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33409947/ — medium (use "roughly 1 in 7")
- India risk factors in that review: age, BMI, non-veg diet, tea/coffee, tobacco, alcohol — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33409947/ — medium
- Indian consensus: <10% of GERD patients in India have erosive esophagitis (many have symptoms with normal-looking esophagus) — https://researcher.manipal.edu/en/publications/indian-consensus-on-gastroesophageal-reflux-disease-in-adults-a-p/ — medium
- NIDDK: about 20% of people in US have GERD — https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/acid-reflux-ger-gerd-adults/definition-facts — high

## Normal physiology
- Parietal cells in upper stomach glands make hydrochloric acid via H+/K+ ATPase proton pump; stomach pH ~1.5-3.5 — https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/proton-pump-inhibitors (pump) / general physiology refs (NCBI Bookshelf blocked; pH from secondary refs) — medium
- Mucus coating shields stomach lining from the acid; the esophagus has no such thick coat — secondary physiology refs — medium
- Lower esophageal sphincter (LES) is a ring of muscle that keeps swallowed food/acid from going back up; reflux = it doesn't close fully — https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000265.htm — high

## Causes / risk factors
- LES weakens or relaxes when it shouldn't — https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/acid-reflux-ger-gerd-adults/symptoms-causes — high
- Hiatal hernia, obesity, pregnancy, smoking/secondhand smoke — same NIDDK + MedlinePlus — high
- Lying down within 3 hours of eating; alcohol — https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000265.htm — high
- Medicines: NSAIDs, calcium channel blockers, benzodiazepines, tricyclics, some asthma drugs, beta-blockers, sedatives — NIDDK + MedlinePlus — high
- NSAID/aspirin users: OR 1.46 for GERD; obesity raises odds significantly — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7118109 — high
- Smoking link in the global meta-analysis was NOT statistically significant (OR 1.04) though guidelines still advise quitting — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7118109 — disputed
- Stress/anxiety, hormones, coffee, tomato, chocolate, fatty/spicy food can trigger; ulcer or bacterial (H. pylori) stomach infection can cause similar symptoms — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heartburn-and-acid-reflux/ — high
- Trigger foods vary per person; evidence is weak/individual (NIDDK cites one 2014 review) — https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/acid-reflux-ger-gerd-adults/eating-diet-nutrition — medium

## What happens step by step
- LES relaxes/weakens -> stomach contents (acid, sometimes food/bile) flow up -> acid touches esophagus lining (not built for acid) -> burning behind breastbone (heartburn) -> repeated exposure -> inflammation (esophagitis) — NIDDK + MedlinePlus — high

## Symptoms
- Heartburn (burning behind breastbone), regurgitation (sour/food taste), nausea, chest pain — NIDDK — high
- Burping, bloating, hiccups, hoarse voice, bad breath, cough; worse after meals, lying down, bending — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heartburn-and-acid-reflux/ — high
- Not everyone with GERD has heartburn; may present as chronic cough/hoarseness/asthma-like symptoms/enamel erosion — NIDDK — high

## Red flags
- Trouble or pain swallowing, persistent vomiting, unexplained weight loss, loss of appetite — NIDDK + NHS + MedlinePlus — high
- Vomit with blood or coffee-ground look; black tarry stool or blood in stool — NIDDK — high
- Feeling full quickly, choking, shortness of breath, food stuck behind breastbone — MedlinePlus — high
- Symptoms not improving with OTC medicine/lifestyle, or heartburn most days — NHS/NIDDK — high
- Heart attack can feel like indigestion/burning chest; chest pain that is tight/squeezing or spreads to arm/neck/jaw = emergency — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heart-attack/symptoms/ — high
- Anaemia as a red flag (from slow bleeding) — standard clinical guidance, not in fetched pages — medium

## Prevention / lifestyle
- Smaller, more frequent meals; don't eat within 3-4 h of bedtime (NHS); at least 3 h (NIDDK) — high
- Raise head of bed 10-20 cm (blocks); extra pillows not recommended — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heartburn-and-acid-reflux/ — high
- Lose weight if overweight; avoid tight waist clothing; quit smoking; limit alcohol — NHS/NIDDK — high

## Treatment
- Antacids: quick, short relief, take after meals/bedtime, not for daily long use unless doctor approves; side effects diarrhoea/constipation — https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/acid-reflux-ger-gerd-adults/treatment — high
- H2 blockers (famotidine): lower acid, heal esophagus less well than PPIs — NIDDK — high
- PPIs (omeprazole, pantoprazole, lansoprazole): work better than H2; usually 4-8 weeks from GP in UK; long-term use only with doctor — NIDDK/NHS — high
- PPI timing: many are taken on empty stomach 30-60 min before a meal (check label/doctor) — https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/proton-pump-inhibitors — medium
- Long-term PPI risk: possible C. diff infection; effects of long-term high dose still studied (other claims like fractures/B12 are debated) — NIDDK — medium/disputed
- Surgery: fundoplication (wrap top of stomach around esophagus end) only if meds fail or patient wants off long-term drugs; more complication risk than medicines — NIDDK — high
- H. pylori: tested and treated with antibiotics + PPI — NHS — high

## Complications if neglected
- Esophagitis (ulcers, bleeding), stricture (narrowing, trouble swallowing), Barrett's esophagus (intestine-like lining); a small number develop esophageal adenocarcinoma — https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/acid-reflux-ger-gerd-adults/definition-facts — high
- Chronic cough, hoarseness, laryngitis, asthma worsening, tooth enamel erosion — NIDDK — high

## Myths vs facts
- "Milk cures acidity": not addressed by NIDDK/NHS/MedlinePlus; relief, if any, is temporary (milk fat/calcium may even trigger more acid/relaxation) — no authoritative support — disputed
- "Acidity = too much acid": reflux is mainly acid in the wrong place (LES problem); PPIs reduce acid but cause is the valve — NIDDK/MedlinePlus — high
- "Spicy food is the only cause": many causes (weight, hernia, meal timing, meds, smoking, stress); triggers vary per person — NIDDK/NHS — high
- "Cold drinks/soda help": no authoritative support; fizzy drinks/alcohol/caffeine commonly listed as triggers (NIDDK lists coffee, alcohol, mint, citrus, fat) — medium
- Antacids treat the cause: no, short-term symptom relief only — NHS — high

## Angle
- Core twist: heartburn is usually not "too much acid" — it is normal, useful acid (pH ~1.5-3.5) in the wrong place because a small muscle valve (LES) fails; the stomach is protected by mucus, the esophagus is not.
- ~1 in 7 people have it (worldwide ~14%, India ~14-15%), yet most never get tested; tablets just hide the symptom while the valve problem (weight, late dinners, lying down) continues.
- Misconception to correct: milk/cold drinks/antacid-for-years fixes it; and "it's just gas" — chest burning can be a heart attack, so know red flags.
