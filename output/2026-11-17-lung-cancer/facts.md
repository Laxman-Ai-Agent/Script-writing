# Lung Cancer
- Globally ~2.5 million new cases and ~1.8 million deaths in 2022; #1 cancer in cases and deaths (12.4% of new cases, 18.7% of cancer deaths) — https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/lung-cancer ; https://www.uicc.org/news/globocan-2022-latest-global-cancer-data-shows-rising-incidence-and-stark-inequities — high (WHO + GLOBOCAN/IARC, 2022 data)
- India 2022: ~81,700 new lung cancer cases and ~75,000 deaths (deaths nearly equal cases) — https://gco.iarc.who.int/media/globocan/factsheets/populations/356-india-fact-sheet.pdf — high
- Lung cancer starts when DNA damage changes how airway-lining cells grow and divide; uncontrolled growth forms a tumour — https://www.cancer.gov/types/lung/patient/non-small-cell-lung-treatment-pdq — high
- Spread (stage IV) to brain, adrenal glands, liver, bones, distant lymph nodes, kidneys — https://www.cancer.gov/types/lung/patient/non-small-cell-lung-treatment-pdq — high
- Two types: NSCLC ~85% (WHO; ACS US figure ~77%), SCLC less common (~13-15%) and more aggressive — https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/lung-cancer ; https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/lung-cancer/about/key-statistics.html — high (use "about 85%" / "most")
- Tobacco smoking causes 60-70% of preventable lung cancer cases (WHO); next are air pollution and occupational exposure — https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/lung-cancer — high
- Other risks: secondhand smoke, radon, asbestos, silica, diesel exhaust, arsenic/chromium/nickel at work, outdoor + household air pollution, chest radiation, family history, HIV — https://www.cancer.gov/types/lung/patient/non-small-cell-lung-treatment-pdq ; https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/lung-cancer — high
- Symptoms: persistent/worsening cough, chest pain, breathlessness, wheeze, coughing blood, hoarseness, weight loss, fatigue, recurring lung infections; early disease may have none or be mistaken for common problems — https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/lung-cancer ; https://www.cancer.gov/types/lung/patient/non-small-cell-lung-treatment-pdq — high
- "Cough > 3 weeks" is the common clinical rule of thumb (not stated on the WHO/NCI pages fetched) — general clinical guidance (NHS/Indian TB programme use 2-3 weeks) — medium
- India: adenocarcinoma ~53% of lung cancers in women; most frequent type in non-smokers (ICMR-NCDIR, via Lok Sabha reply Dec 2025) — https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/3017762/1/AU2282_Iig48T.pdf — medium (secondary summary of ICMR data)
- India: share of never-smokers among lung cancer patients varies by study, from ~24% to 40-50%+; Indians who never smoked are diagnosed at younger ages than Western counterparts — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9894269 ; https://theindianpractitioner.com/non-smoking-indians-diagnosed-with-lung-cancer-earlier-than-western-counterparts — disputed (numbers vary; say "a large share")
- India: lung cancer often mistaken for TB; single-centre study 14.1% (69 patients) wrongly given anti-TB treatment, delaying diagnosis ~4 months; reviews quote 17-42% — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9894269 ; https://outlookindia.com/healthcare-spotlight/indias-silent-storm-lung-cancer-emerges-as-a-wider-threat-beyond-smoking — medium (range wide; say "many patients")
- India 5-year survival estimated ~3.7% vs 21.2% in USA (Lancet-based summary) — https://outlookindia.com/healthcare-spotlight/indias-silent-storm-lung-cancer-emerges-as-a-wider-threat-beyond-smoking — disputed (secondary source, old data; avoid or hedge)
- US 5-year survival NSCLC (2015-2021): localized 67%, regional 40%, distant 12%, all stages 32%. SCLC (2012-2018): localized 34%, regional 20%, distant 4% — https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/lung-cancer/detection-diagnosis-staging/survival-rates.html — high (US data; early stage = about 5x better than distant)
- US 2026 estimate: ~229,410 new cases, ~124,990 deaths; lifetime risk ~1 in 19 incl. non-smokers — https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/lung-cancer/about/key-statistics.html — high
- Diagnosis: chest X-ray, CT, PET-CT, biopsy (needle, bronchoscopy), molecular/biomarker tests (EGFR etc.), brain MRI/bone scan for staging — https://www.cancer.gov/types/lung/patient/non-small-cell-lung-treatment-pdq — high
- Low-dose CT screening (USPSTF): age 50-80, >=20 pack-years, current smoker or quit within 15 years; stop at 81 or 15 years after quitting. Risks: false positives, overdiagnosis, radiation. 1 pack-year = 1 pack/day for 1 year — https://www.cdc.gov/lung-cancer/screening/index.html — high (US guideline; India has no national screening programme, medium)
- Treatment: surgery (wedge, segment, lobectomy, pneumonectomy), chemo, radiation, targeted therapy (EGFR drugs: gefitinib, erlotinib, osimertinib), immunotherapy (pembrolizumab, nivolumab, atezolizumab, durvalumab); choice depends on type, stage, gene tests — https://www.cancer.gov/types/lung/patient/non-small-cell-lung-treatment-pdq ; https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/lung-cancer — high
- Quitting: CDC says added lung cancer risk about halves 10-15 years after quitting; ACS-based figures say lung cancer death risk about half within 10 years; former smokers stay above never-smokers; quitting after age 50 still cut risk ~39% in one study — https://www.cdc.gov/tobacco/about/cigarettes-and-cancer.html ; https://www.cancer.org/cancer/risk-prevention/tobacco/benefits-of-quitting-smoking-over-time.html ; https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/amp/articles/risk-of-lung-cancer-significantly-decreases-after-smoking-is-stopped — medium (timing differs 10 vs 10-15 yrs; say "about 10 years or more")
- WHO: "Not smoking tobacco is the best way to prevent lung cancer"; screening heavy smokers cuts deaths in well-resourced systems — https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/lung-cancer — high
- Myths: biopsy spreading cancer, light/filter cigarettes safe, e-cigs safe, quitting late useless — no dedicated source fetched; use general knowledge (quitting benefits above refute "late is useless"; no evidence biopsy spreads cancer; no safe tobacco form) — medium (verify wording before stating strong claims on e-cigs/biopsy)
- Diet during treatment: general guidance only (small frequent meals, protein, hydration; follow oncologist/dietitian) — general clinical practice, not sourced here — medium
- Red flags needing urgent care: coughing up large amounts of blood, severe breathlessness, sudden chest pain — general emergency guidance — medium
- Bidi: no dedicated source fetched; bidi smoke is tobacco smoke and a known lung cancer risk in India — general knowledge — medium

## Angle
- Core insight: lung cancer is silent early (no pain in lungs, symptoms mimic cough/TB), so it is found late; survival is ~67% if caught localized vs ~12% once spread, and in India deaths (~75k) almost equal cases (~82k). A "TB-like cough that doesn't go away" deserves a CT/biopsy, not just repeated antibiotics.
- Twist: it is no longer only a smoker's disease; in India a large share are never-smokers, especially women, linked to air pollution and cooking/secondhand smoke.
- Misconceptions to correct: "only smokers get it", "quitting late is useless" (risk falls steadily, ~half in 10-15 years), "light/filter cigarettes and e-cigs are safe", "biopsy spreads cancer", "every long cough is TB".
