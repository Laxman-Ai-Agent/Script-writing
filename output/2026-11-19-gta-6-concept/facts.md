## Origin and company
- 1997 first GTA by DMA Design (Dundee, Scotland); began as "Race'n'Chase" police-chase game, devs found it dull, switched to playing the criminal — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Theft_Auto_(video_game) — high
- Rockstar Games founded 1998 by Sam and Dan Houser (with Terry Donovan, Jamie King, Gary Foreman), owned by Take-Two; DMA Design became Rockstar North in 2002 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockstar_Games — medium-high
- Dan Houser left Rockstar in 2020, founded Absurd Ventures, building open-world games; says he is deliberately avoiding watching GTA 6 reveals — https://games.gg/news/dan-houser-avoiding-gta-6-reveal/ — medium (one outlet)

## Pillar 1: interactive cinema
- Houser (as summarised from interviews) said Rockstar borrowed cinematography/cinematic feel from film but not much of its structure; compared games to early cinema (around 1920) and "interactive TV shows" — https://www.alistdaily.com/media/houser-on-gta — medium (paraphrase, not verbatim)
- Houser said adapting GTA to film is hard because it means condensing, not expanding (GTA III 10th anniversary interview) — https://gamespot.com/articles/dan-houser-opens-up-about-grand-theft-auto-iii/1100-6341347/ — medium

## Angle
- Core twist: GTA began as a boring police-chase game; the team flipped it to "play the criminal" and that accident became a formula: cinema-style story + total freedom + satire of America + a world that feels alive. Rockstar's goal is not "a crime game" but a playable satirical movie. Even Houser says adapting it to film is hard because the game is the point.
- Misconception to correct: "GTA glorifies crime" — Houser frames it as satire of media and society; and the user's "99 Metacritic / $1.65B pre-orders" are not confirmed (no Metascore found; pre-orders reported ~$1.4-1.5B).
