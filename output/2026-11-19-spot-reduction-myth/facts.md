# Spot reduction myth
- Meta-analysis (Ramirez-Campillo et al., Human Movement 2022): 13 studies, 1,158 people (age 14-71), 37 comparisons; pooled effect -0.03 (95% CI -0.10 to 0.05, p=0.508) = no evidence that training a muscle burns fat over that muscle — https://hummov.awf.wroc.pl/A-proposed-model-to-test-the-hypothesis-of-exercise-induced-localized-fat-reduction,143162,0,2.html (DOI 10.5114/HM.2022.110373) — high (fetched)
- Vispute, Smith, LeCheminant, Hurley 2011, J Strength Cond Res 25(9):2559-64: 24 sedentary adults, 7 ab exercises, 5 days/week, 6 weeks; no change in body weight, body composition or abdominal circumference; ab endurance improved — https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0b013e3181fb4a46 — medium (details from secondary sources, paper not opened; verify)
- Ramirez-Campillo 2013 (non-dominant leg training 12 weeks): leg fat did not drop in the trained leg; trunk/arm fat dropped (whole-body effect) — https://www.nsca.com/contentassets/60c4deb55cd640549806ed8f3f170b9e/trainertips_spot-reduction.pdf — medium (secondary NSCA summary; verify original)
- Scientific consensus: spot reduction is not achievable; fat is lost from the whole body — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spot_reduction — medium (secondary; the meta-analysis above is the real basis)
- Crunches still strengthen core muscles (back support, posture) but do not specifically melt belly fat — secondary articles only — medium (verify; avoid strong claims about back pain)
- Dissenting: 2023 RCT in adult males reports abdominal endurance exercise used more local fat than treadmill running — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10680576/ — disputed (single small study, contradicts meta-analysis; do not use as fact)
- Why it matters: visceral (deep belly) fat is linked to higher risk of heart disease, diabetes and some cancers; may release inflammatory chemicals that promote insulin resistance — https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/healthy-weight/measuring-fat/ (Harvard T.H. Chan) — high (fetched)
- Waist thresholds: NIH expert panel — waist over 40 in (men) / 35 in (women) raises risk of heart disease and other chronic diseases; Harvard page also gives WHO waist-hip ratio >0.90 men / >0.85 women, waist-to-height >=0.5 — https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/healthy-weight/measuring-fat/ — high (fetched)
- South Asians/Indians: IDF uses waist >=90 cm men / >=80 cm women for abdominal obesity in South Asians (lower than Western cut-offs) — https://doi.org/10.1038/SJ.IJO.0803111 (Misra et al. 2006) — medium (found via search only, IDF page not opened; verify. Indian studies suggest even lower, 78/72 cm; no single agreed Indian value)
- Newer Indian obesity definition (2025): BMI >23 plus excess waist or waist-to-height ratio — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39814628/ — medium (verify before use)
## Angle
- Twist: 13 studies, 1,158 people, and working one body part did not burn fat from that part. Fat comes off the whole body; you cannot aim it.
- Misconception: "100 crunches a day = flat tummy". Crunches build ab muscle, not fat loss over it.
- Takeaway (non-medical): overall fat loss comes from the whole picture (whole-body activity, food, sleep); crunches are fine for core strength. Track waist, not just weight.
## Myths vs facts
- ❌ Crunches burn belly fat ✅ Meta-analysis of 13 studies found no spot reduction
- ❌ Ab exercise shrinks the waist in weeks ✅ A 6-week ab program (24 adults) did not change waist size or body fat
- ❌ Fat burns from the muscle you use ✅ Fat comes from the whole body
- ❌ Core exercise is useless ✅ It builds core strength, just not targeted fat loss
- ❌ Only scale weight matters ✅ Waist size (visceral fat) is a health marker; South Asian cut-off is lower (90/80 cm, verify)
