# Antikythera Mechanism – how was a computer-like machine made thousands of years ago?
- Hand-powered Greek analogue computer; models Sun, Moon positions, eclipses, 4-year games cycle; oldest known analogue computer — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism — high
- Found 1900 by Symi sponge divers (Capt. Dimitrios Kontos) on a Roman-era cargo wreck off Antikythera; mechanism recovered 1901 from ~45 m depth — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism — high
- Wreck dated ~70–60 BC (1st century BC); how the device got on board is unknown — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism ; https://archaeology.org/news/2025/04/15/new-study-suggests-antikythera-mechanism-had-design-flaws/ — high
- Gear noticed in rock lump on 17 May 1902 by archaeologist Valerios Stais, after ~2 years unnoticed in Athens museum — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism — high
- Space.com/Archaeology.org: pieces recovered 1901, nearly 2,000 years old — https://www.space.com/gravitational-waves-lunar-year-cosmic-clock-antikythera-mechanism — high
- 82 fragments total (3 main); 4 contain gears — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism — high
- At least 30 bronze gears surviving; CT suggests ~35 meshing gears originally; largest gear ~13 cm, 223 teeth; teeth are triangular, ~1.6 mm pitch — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism — medium (count of original gears is an estimate)
- Case about 34 x 18 x 9 cm, wooden box with front/back doors carrying inscriptions (back door like a manual); hand crank is lost — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism — medium (size estimated)
- Construction date estimates: ~205 BC, ~150–100 BC, ~87 BC; calibration date proposals 178 BC (2022 study) vs 204 BC — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism — disputed
- Maker unknown; proposals: Rhodes (Hipparchus/Posidonius), Syracuse (Archimedes' school), Pergamon, Corinthian colonies; Corinthian-style month names, calendar matches Epirus/Corfu — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism — disputed
- Front: fixed zodiac ring (12 x 30 deg) + movable Egyptian-style calendar ring; sun/moon pointers; moon-phase ball driven by differential gear — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism — high
- Moon pointer uses pin-and-slot epicyclic gear to mimic Moon's uneven speed (lunar anomaly) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism — high
- Back dials: Metonic (235 months/19 yrs, 5 spiral turns), Saros (223 months ~18 yrs; glyphs for 38 lunar + 27 solar eclipses), Games dial (4-year cycle, Olympic/Isthmian etc.; renamed from "Olympiad" dial), Callippic (76 yrs), Exeligmos (triple Saros, adds 0/8/16 hours) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism — high
- Nothing comparable until 14th-century European astronomical clocks (~1,400+ years gap) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism — high
- Research: 1951 Derek de Solla Price began study; 1971 X-ray/gamma-ray with Karakalos; 2002 Michael Wright working model; 2005 Cardiff-led (Mike Edmunds) X-ray CT revealed hidden inscriptions — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism — high
- 2021 UCL (Tony Freeth) proposed full planetary model (Sun, Moon, 5 planets) using shared gears with factors 7 and 17; planets' pointers shown as theory, model not physically proven — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism ; https://www.space.com/gravitational-waves-lunar-year-cosmic-clock-antikythera-mechanism (mentions 2021 X-ray findings + planets inscriptions) — medium (planet gearing is a proposal, original planet gears lost)
- June 2024 Glasgow (Woan & Bayley, Horological Journal): calendar ring most likely 354–355 holes (lunar year), not 365; used gravitational-wave statistics (MCMC, nested sampling) — https://www.gla.ac.uk/news/archiveofnews/2024/june/headline_1086643_en.html ; https://www.space.com/gravitational-waves-lunar-year-cosmic-clock-antikythera-mechanism — high for the finding, disputed for meaning (Freeth: "just wrong"; Edmunds doubts it was a lunar calendar) (source: WebSearch summary of Smithsonian/artnet coverage)
- Earlier Budiselic estimate: 347–367 holes — https://www.gla.ac.uk/news/archiveofnews/2024/june/headline_1086643_en.html — high
- April 2025 arXiv preprint (Szigety & Arenas): triangular teeth + manufacturing errors may cause jamming; model said could be cranked only ~4 months ahead before reset; authors say speculative, measurements may not match original — https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00327 ; https://archaeology.org/news/2025/04/15/new-study-suggests-antikythera-mechanism-had-design-flaws/ — medium (preprint, not peer reviewed)
- Freeth & Jones: Mars pointer error up to ~38 deg due to Greek planetary theory, not gearing — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism — medium
- Now held in National Archaeological Museum, Athens — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism — high

## Angle
- Core insight: a 2,000-year-old box of hand-cut bronze gears predicted eclipses and Moon's uneven speed; then the technology seems to vanish for ~1,400 years, so it hints at lost Greek engineering; and even today we cannot say who built it, or whether it ran perfectly.
- Misconception: it was not a "computer" in the modern sense nor a "clock/GPS"; it was an astronomical calculator (calendar + eclipse predictor). It was not found with a known maker (not proven Archimedes), and the ship (70-60 BC) is younger than the device. Also "oldest computer" is a label, not a literal claim.
