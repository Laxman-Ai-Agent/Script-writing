# Deepest Ocean – Challenger Deep
- Deepest known point on Earth = Challenger Deep, southern end of Mariana Trench, western Pacific — https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/oceandepth.html — high
- NOAA depth figure ~10,935 m (35,876 ft); other measurements range ~10,900–10,994 m (2019 Vescovo sensors avg ~10,925 m; peak 10,928 m) — https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/oceandepth.html ; https://www.boatinternational.com/yachts/news/eyos-expeditions-five-deeps-mission-successfully-dives-to-deepest-point-on-earth--38675 ; https://GuinnessWorldRecords.com/records/hall-of-fame/Victor-Vescovo-Deepest-dive-by-a-crewed-vessel — medium (range ~10.9–11 km; say "about 11 km" in script)
- Ocean average depth ~3,682 m (so Challenger Deep ~3x average) — https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/oceandepth.html — high
- Named after HMS Challenger, whose crew first sounded the trench in 1875 — https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/oceandepth.html — high
- 23 Jan 1960: Don Walsh and Jacques Piccard in bathyscaphe Trieste, first to reach the bottom — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_who_descended_to_Challenger_Deep ; https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/620890-first-person-to-reach-the-lowest-points-of-earths-oceans — high
- 25 Mar 2012: James Cameron, solo, Deepsea Challenger — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_who_descended_to_Challenger_Deep — high
- 28 Apr 2019: Victor Vescovo, DSV Limiting Factor (Five Deeps expedition); new deepest crewed dive record (~10,928 m) — https://www.livescience.com/65468-explorer-breaks-record-deepest-ocean-dive.html ; https://www.nbcnews.com/mach/science/explorer-reaches-bottom-mariana-trench-sets-new-record-deepest-dive-ncna1005211 — high
- 7 Jun 2020: Kathryn Sullivan (first American woman to spacewalk) reached bottom, first person to be in space and the deepest ocean point; Vanessa O'Brien 12 Jun 2020 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_who_descended_to_Challenger_Deep — high
- 10 Nov 2020: Chinese submersible Fendouzhe (Striver) with 3 crew (Zhang Wei, Zhao Yang, Wang Zhiqiang) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_who_descended_to_Challenger_Deep — medium (listed on Wikipedia only; depth ~10,909 m widely reported)
- Total: 27 distinct people, 22 successful dives by last list update (last dive listed 12 Jul 2022; 19 of 22 dives used Limiting Factor) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_who_descended_to_Challenger_Deep — medium (single source; verify nothing newer before publishing; say "around 27")
- Famous line "more people have walked on the Moon (12) than reached Challenger Deep" is now OUT OF DATE: 27 > 12 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_who_descended_to_Challenger_Deep — medium (12 Moon-walkers is well established; 27 from Wikipedia list)
- Pressure at bottom ~1,086 bar ≈ 15,750 psi ≈ ~1,071 times sea-level atmospheric pressure (ie >1,000 atm) — https://www.worldatlas.com/oceans/the-deepest-point-in-the-ocean.html ; https://EN.wikipedia.com/wiki/Mariana_Trench — high (as physics, ~1,000+ atm; exact value varies with depth used)
- Relatable comparison: ~100 adult elephants standing on your head (UCL explainer); roughly 1 tonne on every square cm — https://www.ucl.ac.uk/engage/case-studies/2021/apr/how-much-pressure-builds-deepest-point-ocean — medium (elephant analogy is illustrative; 1,071 atm x ~1 kg/cm2 = ~1.1 tonne/cm2 is own arithmetic)
- Mariana snailfish (Pseudoliparis swirei) named 2017, caught at ~7,966 m — https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/deepest-sea-fish-identified-snailfish-pseudoliparis-swirei-spd — high
- Deepest fish ever filmed: juvenile Pseudoliparis snailfish at 8,336 m, Izu-Ogasawara Trench (Japan), announced Apr 2023 (UWA + Tokyo Univ. of Marine Science and Technology); not in Mariana — https://nhm.ac.uk/discover/news/2023/april/deepest-ever-fish-filmed-depth-8336-metres.html ; https://www.npr.org/2023/04/05/1168051498/deepest-fish-japan-photograph ; https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/deepest-ever-fish-rcna77858 — high
- Two snailfish (P. belyaevi) trapped at 8,022 m in Japan Trench = first fish collected below 8,000 m — https://www.npr.org/2023/04/05/1168051498/deepest-fish-japan-photograph — high
- Fish limit theory: below ~8,200–8,400 m fish cannot survive (body chemistry, TMAO); no fish seen in Challenger Deep, so the deepest floor has no fish — https://e360.yale.edu/digest/deepest-fish-recorded-snailfish-japan ; https://www.livescience.com/deepest-dwelling-fish-ever-seen-is-a-ghostly-snailfish-spotted-more-than-27000-feet-beneath-the-ocean-surface — medium (theory; sources vary on exact limit; a claimed fish below 10,000 m was never confirmed)
- Amphipods (Hirondellea gigas, Eurythenes) thrive at Challenger Deep; 2019 Royal Society Open Science: every animal sampled at 10,890 m had microplastic in gut — https://doaj.org/article/3b36623a3fa64a52b302c9ee204b1ca5 ; https://www.nationalgeographic.com/environment/2019/02/deep-sea-creatures-mariana-trench-eat-plastic — high
- Persistent pollutants (PCBs, PBDEs) found in trench amphipods at very high levels (Nature Ecology & Evolution, Jamieson 2017) — https://www.nationalgeographic.com/environment/2019/02/deep-sea-creatures-mariana-trench-eat-plastic ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6408374/ — high

## Angle
- Twist: we know the bottom of Challenger Deep better than ever (27 people, a 2020 spacewalker, Cameron alone) yet >70% of the seafloor is still unmapped in modern detail; the deepest place is crowded with visits but also with our plastic and pollutants
- Twist 2: life gets there anyway (amphipods with microplastic) while fish give up at ~8,300 m
- Misconception: "more people have walked on the Moon than visited Challenger Deep" — no longer true (27 vs 12); also the "11 km / 10,994 m" depth varies by measurement (10.9-11 km), not a single exact number
- Misconception: pressure "crushes" life — animals there are adapted; it is our subs that need titanium/syntactic foam
