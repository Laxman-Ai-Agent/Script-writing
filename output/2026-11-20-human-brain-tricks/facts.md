# Human Brain – how our brain fools us without us knowing
- Adult male brain has ~86.1 billion neurons (±8.1) and ~84.6 billion non-neuronal cells; old "10 glia per neuron" claim was never measured — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19226510/ (Azevedo 2009, J Comp Neurol); also https://bionumbers.hms.harvard.edu/bionumber.aspx?id=106399 — high
- Brain is ~2% of body weight but uses ~20% of resting energy (infants ~50%) — https://humanorigins.si.edu/education/fun-facts/our-big-hungry-brain ; https://www.quantamagazine.org/how-much-energy-does-it-take-to-think-20250604/ ; https://bionumbers.hms.harvard.edu/bionumber.aspx?id=100723 — high
- Each neuron has ~7,000 synapses on average; total brain synapses estimated 100–500 trillion (">100 trillion" in other source) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuron ; https://aips.org.au/news-events/why-brains-are-so-energy-hungry — medium (estimate, wide range)
- Neurons fire all-or-nothing electrical pulses (action potentials); stronger stimulus = higher firing rate, not bigger spike; chemical signal at synapse — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuron — high
- Signal speed: myelinated axons up to ~120 m/s (some sources 150 m/s); unmyelinated <2 to ~10 m/s — https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK10921 ; https://open.lib.umn.edu/sensationandperception/chapter/conduction-velocity-and-myelin/ — high (upper bound varies 120–150)
- Synapses often fail: a signal triggers release only ~20% of the time — https://www.quantamagazine.org/how-much-energy-does-it-take-to-think-20250604/ — medium
- Blind spot: optic nerve exits retina at optic disc with no photoreceptors; ~7.5° high x 5.5° wide in each eye; brain fills it in from surroundings and the other eye — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blind_spot_(vision) — high
- Invisible gorilla (Simons & Chabris 1999, Perception 28:1059): in most groups ~50% did not report the gorilla while counting passes — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inattentional_blindness ; https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1068/p281059 (paper) — high (exact % varies by condition; harder task = more misses)
- Eye-tracking: people can look directly at unexpected object and still miss it (Memmert: ~1 second fixation) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inattentional_blindness — medium
- Choice blindness (Johansson, Hall et al., Science 2005; 120 participants, 15 face pairs): on swapped-photo trials only ~26% noticed (some outlets say 13%); people then gave reasons for a choice they never made (confabulation); 84% said beforehand they would notice — https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/when-free-choice-is-an-illusion/ ; https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/why-did-i-do — medium (detection % differs by source: 13% vs 26%; say "most people did not notice")
- Loftus & Pickrell 1995 "lost in the mall": 24 participants, ~25% (6 of 24) accepted the implanted false childhood memory; meant as existence proof, not population rate — https://www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_in_the_mall_technique ; https://forrt.org/flora-replication-atlas/doi/10.3928/0048-5713-19951201-07/ — high for the 25% in this sample
- 2023 replication (Murphy et al.) coded ~35% with false memory; a reanalysis argues many "false" memories were partly real (e.g. had been lost before) — https://scientificamerican.com/article/people-likely-arent-as-susceptible-to-false-memories-as-researchers-thought — disputed
- Memory is distributed, not in one spot; engram = physical trace of a memory; 2012 Nature (Tonegawa lab) reactivated mouse fear memory with light; exact storage mechanism and location of engrams still under investigation — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engram_(neuropsychology) — medium/high (field unsolved)
- Each recall is a rebuild, so memories can be altered (reconstructive memory; reconsolidation) — source: Loftus work above; reconsolidation not separately sourced — medium
- Why we dream is unsettled: competing ideas = memory consolidation (REM/sleep replay), threat simulation, overfitting/noise (Hoel), activation-synthesis (Hobson); Freud/Jung largely historical; no theory accepted by most researchers — https://discovermagazine.com/mind/why-do-we-dream-science-offers-a-few-possibilities ; https://journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/index.php/IJoDR/libraryFiles/downloadPublic/890 — disputed
- Hard problem of consciousness (Chalmers, 1995): why/how physical brain activity gives subjective experience (qualia); some think solvable, Dennett calls it a chimera, Pinker "forever beyond grasp" — https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Hard_Problem ; https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-mystery-of-consciousness-is-deeper-than-we-thought/ — disputed
- Perception delay / brain predicts reality (~80 ms lag) and phantom limb: NOT verified in this research; treat as unsourced, say only in general terms ("brain predicts and fills in") or skip — low

## Angle
- Core insight: you never see reality directly; your brain builds a best-guess movie (fills the blind spot, skips what you ignore, rebuilds memories each time, invents reasons for choices) and feels 100% sure. The "tricks" are not bugs but shortcuts to save energy (2% weight, 20% fuel). Ends with: 86 billion neurons and we still can't explain why any of it feels like something (consciousness), why we dream, or exactly how a memory is stored.
- Misconception: "we use only 10% of the brain" is a myth (brain is active throughout; it burns 20% of body energy) — source not fetched here (general neuroscience consensus; cite e.g. Scientific American/Johns Hopkins if used) — high but unsourced above
- Misconception: memory is like a video recording — it is reconstructed on recall; confidence does not equal accuracy
- Misconception: "10 times more glia than neurons" — corrected by Azevedo 2009 (~1:1)
