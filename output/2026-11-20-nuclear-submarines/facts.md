# Nuclear Submarines
- Nautilus (SSN-571) commissioned 1954; left berth 17 Jan 1955 signalling "Underway on nuclear power" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Nautilus_(SSN-571) — high
- Nautilus reached North Pole submerged 3 Aug 1958; 96-hr voyage, ~1,590 nm (2,940 km) under ice — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Nautilus_(SSN-571) , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine — high
- Nautilus top speed ~23 knots; crew 13 officers + 92 enlisted; 1,200 nm submerged in under 90 hrs on shakedown — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Nautilus_(SSN-571) — high
- Nuclear power + oxygen-from-seawater equipment (1950s) let subs stay submerged weeks/months — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine — high
- Reactors are pressurised water reactors (PWR) in US, Russia, UK, France, China, India; boiling water reactors unsuitable (noisy, radioactive circulating water) — https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/non-power-nuclear-applications/transport/nuclear-powered-ships — high
- Reactor heat -> steam -> geared turbines drive shaft (e.g. Ohio: one S8G PWR, two geared turbines 35,000 shp, one shaft; Typhoon: two reactors, 50,000 shp turbines each) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio-class_submarine , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon-class_submarine — high (electricity via turbogenerators is standard; no source fetched stating it explicitly: medium)
- US sub reactors reportedly use highly enriched uranium ~93% U-235 (early ~97%); UK subs use US-supplied HEU; Arihant ~40%; Russian 20-45%; France low-enriched (~5-7.5%) — https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/non-power-nuclear-applications/transport/nuclear-powered-ships — medium (exact enrichment officially secret)
- Most sub cores last 30-40 years without refuelling; Virginia core ~33 yrs, Columbia core lasts whole life; Ohio needs mid-life refuel ~25 yrs; French Rubis refuel every 7-10 yrs — https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/non-power-nuclear-applications/transport/nuclear-powered-ships , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia-class_submarine , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia-class_submarine — high
- Oxygen: electrolysis of purified water (reactor gives enough power); seawater must first be distilled / reverse-osmosis purified (raw salt water electrolysis gives chlorine); oxygen candle (sodium chlorate + iron powder) is backup if power lost — https://www.wearethemighty.com/articles/this-is-how-modern-submarines-do-not-run-out-of-oxygen-while-submerged.md , https://time.com/archive/6622665/science-fresh-air-in-the-depths , https://www.scienceabc.com/eyeopeners/how-do-people-get-oxygen-and-drinking-water-inside-a-submarine — high (candle composition: one blog disagrees, Time version used)
- CO2 removed by scrubbers (amine or soda lime); scrubber reactions can yield water — https://cosmosmagazine.com/technology/oxygen-generators-and-carbon-dioxide-scrubbers-explained , https://www.rtx.com/collinsaerospace/what-we-do/industries/military-and-defense/submarines-and-surface-ships/submarine-life-support-and-atmospheric-monitoring-technology — high
- Drinking water made from seawater by distillation / reverse osmosis — https://www.wearethemighty.com/articles/this-is-how-modern-submarines-do-not-run-out-of-oxygen-while-submerged.md — high
- Virginia-class endurance "only limited by food and maintenance requirements"; range unlimited — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia-class_submarine — high (quoted phrase; no official 90-day figure fetched)
- Ohio class: 16,764 t surfaced / 18,750 t submerged; 560 ft (170 m); crew ~155 (15 officers, 140 enlisted), Blue and Gold crews; test depth 240 m (800 ft); speed officially 20+ knots (reports ~25) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio-class_submarine — depth/speed medium (classified)
- Ohio patrols typically 70-90 days; longest recorded 140 days (USS Pennsylvania, Jan-Jun 2014); 18 built (14 SSBN, 4 SSGN) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio-class_submarine — high
- Columbia class: ~21,140 t submerged, 171 m, 16 Trident D5 tubes, lead boat USS District of Columbia due ~2031, ~$9B each; replaces Ohio — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia-class_submarine — high (schedule slips often)
- Typhoon (Project 941): largest subs ever built, ~48,000 t submerged, 175 m, crew ~160, test depth ~400 m, 2 reactors (HEU up to 45%), autonomy 120+ days (~6 months submerged cited) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon-class_submarine , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine — medium (Russian figures partly estimates)
- Borei class: ~24,000 t submerged, 170 m, crew ~107, test depth ~400 m, ~29 knots, 16 Bulava missiles, 8 active — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borei-class_submarine — medium
- Virginia class: ~7,900 t submerged (Block V ~10,200 t), crew ~135, speed 25+ knots (unconfirmed up to 35), test depth 800+ ft (unconfirmed estimate ~1,600 ft / 490 m) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia-class_submarine — disputed (depth/speed classified)
- ~150 nuclear subs in service or building, down from 400+ at Cold War end; late-2019 counts: US 70, Russia 40, China 19, UK 10, France 9, India 3 — https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/non-power-nuclear-applications/transport/nuclear-powered-ships — medium (counts shift)
- Not fetched/verified: Seawolf, Yasen, Arihant specs beyond Arihant ~40% enrichment (India has 3 nuclear subs per WNA count). Yasen/Seawolf: mention only without numbers.

## Angle
- Core insight: the reactor never runs out; the sub's real limit is food and the crew's patience (~70-90 day patrols; record 140 days). It makes its own air and water from the sea using the reactor's power.
- Twist: Nautilus 1955 "underway on nuclear power" -> a fuel core can last 30+ years (life-of-ship in newest classes), so a boat could in theory circle the planet again and again.
- Misconception: subs don't "store" oxygen for months and aren't limited by fuel; also they don't make oxygen from raw seawater directly — it is purified first, then electrolysed. Depth and speed figures are classified; all public numbers are estimates.
