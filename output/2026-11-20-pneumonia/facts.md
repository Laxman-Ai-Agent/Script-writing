# Pneumonia

## 1) Why it matters
- Pneumonia caused 14% of all deaths in children under 5 in 2019 (about 740,180 children); 22% of deaths in ages 1-5 — WHO — high (single source for the figure; WHO is primary)
- Deaths highest in southern Asia and sub-Saharan Africa (India is in southern Asia) — WHO — high
- Only about one third of children with pneumonia receive the antibiotics they need — WHO — high
- Affects millions of people worldwide each year (no exact figure) — CDC — high
- World Pneumonia Day is November 12 — CDC — high

## 2) Causes and risk factors
- Can be caused by bacteria, viruses or fungi — WHO, CDC, NHLBI, MedlinePlus — high
- Most common bacterial cause in WHO's child fact sheet: Streptococcus pneumoniae; second: Hib — WHO — high
- Other bacteria: Mycoplasma pneumoniae (common in community pneumonia), Chlamydia pneumoniae, Legionella, whooping cough bacteria — CDC — high
- Viruses: RSV (most common in children, WHO), flu, COVID-19, rhinovirus, parainfluenza, metapneumovirus; measles and chickenpox less common — WHO, CDC — high
- Fungi: less common; more often in people with chronic illness / weak immunity — CDC, MedlinePlus — high
- Often follows another infection like flu, RSV or COVID-19; viral pneumonia can be followed by bacterial pneumonia — NHS, MedlinePlus — high
- Aspiration (food/fluid/water entering lungs) can cause pneumonia — NHS, MedlinePlus — high
- Sometimes doctors cannot identify the germ — CDC — high
- Risk: children 2 and under, adults 65+ — MedlinePlus — high (CDC says under 5 and 65+; age cut-off differs slightly between sources — disputed in detail, say "very young and older people")
- Risk: smoking, heavy alcohol, malnutrition, lung disease, weak immune system, trouble coughing/swallowing, recent cold/flu, diabetes, heart failure, sickle cell, liver and kidney disease, chemical/toxic fume exposure, hospital/ICU stay — MedlinePlus, CDC-RF — high
- Child risk: malnutrition (esp. not exclusively breastfed), HIV, measles, indoor air pollution from wood/dung cooking fuel, crowded homes, parental smoking — WHO — high
- Occurs all year but more in fall and winter — CDC-RF — high (so "only in cold weather" is a myth)

## 3) Organ and process (step by step)
- Infection of one or both lungs; the air sacs (alveoli) fill with fluid or pus — NHLBI — high
- Germs reach the lungs by inhaling droplets from nose/throat, from coughs/sneezes; also via blood especially around birth — WHO — high
- Step-by-step: germ is inhaled -> reaches alveoli -> body's immune response causes inflammation -> alveoli fill with fluid/pus -> oxygen exchange drops -> cough, breathlessness, fever — assembled from NHLBI + WHO; immune/inflammation step is general physiology, not stated in detail on fetched pages — medium (ధృవీకరించుకోవాలి for the wording "immune response")
- Low blood oxygen is why severe cases need oxygen therapy — MedlinePlus, NHLBI — high
- Severity depends on age, overall health and cause — NHLBI — high

## 4) Symptoms
- Early/common: cough (with or without mucus), fever and chills, shortness of breath, chest pain on breathing/coughing, fatigue, nausea/vomiting/diarrhea, aching body, loss of appetite, wheezing — CDC, NHS, MedlinePlus — high
- Older adults: may have milder symptoms, lower-than-normal temperature, weakness, sudden confusion — MedlinePlus, NHS — high
- Newborns may show no obvious signs; babies may make grunting noises — MedlinePlus, NHS — high
- Children under 5 with cough/difficult breathing: fast breathing or lower chest wall indrawing — WHO — high
- Wheezing more common with viral infections — WHO — high

## 5) Prevention
- Vaccines (general): pneumococcal, flu, Hib, measles, whooping cough, COVID-19, RSV (eligibility by age/health) — WHO, NHS, MedlinePlus — high
- WHO: immunization is the most effective prevention for children — high
- Exclusive breastfeeding first 6 months, good nutrition — WHO — high
- Clean indoor stoves/cooking fuel, less crowding, handwashing with soap — WHO — high
- Don't smoke — NHS, MedlinePlus — high
- Cover coughs/sneezes, dispose tissues, wash hands — NHS — high

## 6) Lifestyle / food (general only, no cure claims)
- Rest and drink plenty of fluids — NHS — high
- NHS: don't take cough medicine because coughing helps clear infection; paracetamol/ibuprofen only if told you can (do NOT name drugs in script) — NHS — high
- No source fetched makes specific food claims; stay general ("nutrition supports recovery" — ధృవీకరించుకోవాలి, keep soft wording)
- Recovery can take a week to over a month; most improve in 2-4 weeks on antibiotics — NHS, MedlinePlus — high

## 7) Treatment (general categories) and when to see a doctor
- Antibiotics treat bacterial pneumonia (and some fungal types), not viral — MedlinePlus, NHS — high
- Antivirals in some viral cases; antifungals for fungal types — MedlinePlus, NHLBI — high
- Severe cases: hospital care, IV treatment, oxygen — NHLBI, WHO, NHS — high
- Hospital more likely for over-65s, heart/lung conditions, young children, very unwell — NHS — high
- Finish the full course prescribed; see doctor again if no improvement in 2-3 days — NHS — high
- Diagnosis: history, exam, maybe chest X-ray — NHLBI — high
- See doctor if cough persists, yellow/green phlegm, chest pain on breathing, breathlessness — NHS — high
- Most children's cases are treated with oral medicine at health centres — WHO — high

## 8) Complications if neglected
- Bacteremia (bacteria in blood) -> septic shock; lung abscess; pleural disorders (fluid around lungs); kidney failure; respiratory failure — MedlinePlus — high (only one source fetched; Mayo blocked — medium for exact list)

## 9) Danger signs (urgent care)
- Adults: struggling to breathe, pale/blue/blotchy skin, lips or tongue, coughing blood, sudden confusion — NHS — high
- Children: fast breathing, lower chest indrawing, unable to feed or drink, unconsciousness, convulsions, very low temperature in infants — WHO — high
- Babies: can't be woken or floppy — NHS — high
- Worsening symptoms -> see provider right away — NHLBI — high

## Angle
- Core insight: pneumonia is not one disease but a condition (lung infection) caused by many different germs; the lungs' tiny air sacs fill with fluid so oxygen can't pass across. Phrase gently: "air sacs filled with fluid instead of air".
- Twist: for under-5s it is a leading killer (14%, 2019), yet largely preventable with vaccines, clean cooking fuel and nutrition, and treatable with ordinary antibiotics (only 1 in 3 gets them).
- Walking pneumonia: CDC lists Mycoplasma pneumoniae as a common cause; the term "walking pneumonia" is not on the fetched pages — ధృవీకరించుకోవాలి before use; if used, say mild cases may let people keep going.
- Misconception: "it's just a bad cold / cough will go on its own" — a cold is in the upper nose/throat, pneumonia is in the lung air sacs.

## Myths vs facts
- ❌ Pneumonia only comes in cold weather — ✅ More common in fall/winter but can occur any time of year (CDC)
- ❌ Antibiotics always needed — ✅ Only for bacterial (and some fungal); not viral; doctor decides (MedlinePlus)
- ❌ It's just a bad cold — ✅ It is an infection of the lungs' air sacs and can need hospital oxygen (NHLBI)
- ❌ Only old people get it — ✅ Under-5 children and 65+ are higher risk, but anyone can get it; smoking, diabetes etc. raise risk (WHO, CDC)
- ❌ Take cough syrup to stop the cough fast — ✅ NHS says coughing helps clear infection; ask doctor before cough medicine
- ❌ Is it contagious? — ✅ Nuance: pneumonia itself isn't a single germ, but the germs causing it (flu, RSV, COVID, bacteria) spread via droplets; hygiene helps (WHO, NHS). CDC/MedlinePlus pages did not state this directly — medium
