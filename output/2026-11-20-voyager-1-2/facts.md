# Voyager 1 & 2
Sources: [NASA] https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/where-are-voyager-1-and-voyager-2-now/ | [SVS] https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/15120 | [JPL] https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasa-shuts-off-instrument-on-voyager-1-to-keep-spacecraft-operating/ | [W1] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyager_1 | [W2] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyager_2 | [EarthSky] https://earthsky.org/space/voyager-1-1-light-day-from-earth-november-17-18-2026/ | [Sky at Night] https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/news/voyager-1-low-energy-charged-particles-experiment-shut-down
- Voyager 2 launched Aug 20, 1977; Voyager 1 launched Sep 5, 1977 (V1 launched second but is faster), Titan IIIE from Cape Canaveral — W1, W2 — high
- Grand Tour concept (visit all outer planets, aligned once ~175 years) was scaled back by 1972 to "Mariner Jupiter-Saturn" probes; V2 kept option to continue to Uranus/Neptune — W2 — high
- Jupiter flybys: V1 Mar 5, 1979; V2 Jul 9, 1979. Voyager 1 found active volcanoes on Io (first active volcanism seen beyond Earth) — W1, W2 — high
- Saturn: V1 Nov 12, 1980 (~124,000 km, Titan thick atmosphere); V2 Aug 26, 1981 — W1, W2 — high
- Uranus: V2 Jan 24, 1986 (11 new moons, 2 new rings); Neptune: V2 Aug 25, 1989 (new rings, 6 moons, Great Dark Spot, since vanished) — W2 — high
- Triton geysers (nitrogen plumes) discovered by V2 in 1989 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triton_(moon) — medium (not checked in fetched pages; W2 page did not mention)
- Europa cracked icy surface seen 1979 — not verified in fetched pages — medium
- Pale Blue Dot: V1 photo, Feb 14, 1990, family portrait of the solar system — W1 — high
- Golden Record on both probes (greetings, sounds, music, images) — not verified in fetched pages, general NASA knowledge — medium
- V1 crossed heliopause Aug 25, 2012 at ~121 AU, first human-made object in interstellar space; V2 Nov 5, 2018 at ~119.7 AU — W1, W2 — high
- V1 reached exactly one light-day from Earth Nov 18, 2026, 2:16:07 a.m. PST (10:16:07 UTC), ~16.094 billion miles / 25.902 billion km (~173 AU); first human-made object to do so — NASA, SVS, EarthSky — high (2+ sources)
- Command to V1 takes ~24 h to arrive, answer another ~24 h (round trip ~2 days) — SVS — high
- V2 will not reach one light-day until ~Nov 2035 — EarthSky — medium (one source)
- V1 speed ~17 km/s (~61,000 km/h) relative to Sun; V2 ~15.3 km/s — W1, W2 — high
- Nov 2023: V1 flight data subsystem (FDS) gave unusable data; Apr 2024 engineers fixed it by moving code away from a faulty memory chip — W1 — high (widely reported by NASA/JPL; one fetched source)
- Thrusters: V1 trajectory-correction thrusters revived 2017 after unused since 1980; more revived 2018, 2019, 2025 — W1 — medium (2025 detail thin)
- Power: RTGs (plutonium) ~470 W at launch, declining ~4 W/year; engineering data possible until ~2036 — W1 — medium
- Feb 27, 2026: V1 roll maneuver followed by unexpected power drop — Sky at Night — medium (one source)
- Apr 17, 2026: NASA shut off V1 Low-energy Charged Particles (LECP) instrument to save power — JPL, Sky at Night — high (2 sources)
- V2 LECP switched off Mar 2025 — JPL, Sky at Night — high
- Each probe had 10 instruments; 7 shut off in total (JPL phrasing unclear); V1 now has 2 working science instruments (magnetometer + plasma wave) — EarthSky/search summaries — medium
- V2 has 3 science instruments active — W2 — medium
- Data sent now: magnetic field strength and plasma waves/density in interstellar space — W1/W2 — medium

## Disputed / uncertain
- V2 Cosmic Ray Subsystem: W2 diagram says off in 2025; another summary says scheduled for 2026 — disputed, avoid exact date.
- Exact instrument counts left on each probe — medium, say "only a few".

## Angle
- Core insight: V1 is now so far that a signal takes a full day to reach it. It runs on 1970s computers, used a planetary alignment that comes once in ~175 years, and is staying alive by switching off its own instruments one by one.
- Misconception: the Voyagers have not left the solar system (Oort cloud still ahead, ~300 years to reach it); they only left the heliosphere. Also V1 is farther despite launching second.
