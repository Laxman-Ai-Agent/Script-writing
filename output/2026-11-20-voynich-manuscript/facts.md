# Voynich Manuscript
Sources: W = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voynich_manuscript ; Y = https://beinecke.library.yale.edu/collections/highlights/voynich-manuscript ; H = https://www.history.com/articles/voynich-manuscript-mystery ; A = https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/01/07/can-a-new-cipher-help-to-explain-the-mysterious-voynich-manuscript ; S = https://www.sci.news/othersciences/linguistics/voynich-manuscript-cipher-14466.html (Britannica fetch blocked 403)

## Basics
- Yale Beinecke MS 408; unidentified script, unknown author — Y — high
- Size ~23.5 x 16.2 x 5 cm; vellum (calfskin, 2014 protein test; ~14-15 whole calfskins) — W — high
- ~240 pages survive of est. ~272 (18 of 20 quires); at least 14 folios (28 pages) missing — W — high
- Sections (pages): Herbal 126, Astronomical 17, Balneological (bathing women, pipes) 20, Cosmological (medallions, foldout) 14, Pharmaceutical 16, Recipes/text 25, plus 8 text-only — W/Y — high
- Herbal section ~half the book; plants part realistic, part imaginary; no confirmed plant ID — H, W — medium
- Text: 170,000+ characters; ~37,919 words; 8,114 unique words (Sabar 2024: ~38,000 / ~9,000) — W — high
- Almost no words shorter than 2 or longer than 10 letters — W — high
- Follows Zipf's law (top word ~2x second, ~3x third); word entropy ~10 bits (similar to English/Latin) — W — high
- But character-level conditional entropy h2 ~2 vs 3-4 in natural languages (Bowern & Lindemann) = very repetitive/predictable letter patterns — W — high
- Handwriting analysis (Lisa Fagin Davis, 2020): 5 scribes — W, H — medium-high

## Dating and history
- University of Arizona radiocarbon 2009: vellum 1404-1438 — W — high (History.com says 2011 - treat as error, use 2009)
- First confirmed owner: Georg Baresch, Prague alchemist; 1639 letter to Athanasius Kircher = earliest confirmed mention — W — high
- Jan Marek Marci sent it to Kircher 1665/1666 with letter: said it was once Rudolf II's, bought for 600 ducats, and attributed to Roger Bacon — W, Y — high (the hearsay itself is unverified)
- Faint signature Jacobus Horcicky de Tepenec (Rudolf's botanist, Sinapius) on first page; match disputed; Yale lists his library before Baresch — W, Y — medium/disputed
- Book stayed with Jesuits (Collegio Romano); Voynich bought 1912 (Yale: from Jesuit College at Frascati / Villa Mondragone) — Y, W — high
- Ethel Voynich left it to Anne Nill (1960); Nill sold to H.P. Kraus 1961; Kraus donated to Yale 1969 — W, Y — high
- Left Beinecke once: Folger exhibition 2014-15; full scans online (hi-res scans first 2004; full online 2020 per W); Yale Univ Press facsimile 2016 — Y, W — high
- Roger Bacon (1214-94) authorship: dead on dating (book is ~150 years after him) — W — high

## Decoding attempts
- Friedman (1940s-50s) and NSA/US codebreakers failed; Friedman guessed an artificial/universal language; Tiltman (asked 1950) called it "a cumbersome mixture of different kinds of substitution"; Elizebeth Friedman 1962: statistical approaches "doomed to utter frustration" — W — high
- Newbold 1921: claimed micro-marks in letters hid a code; refuted by Manly 1931; marks = ink cracking — W — high
- Bax 2014: partial decoding of ~10 proper nouns, 14 letters; died 2017, no full translation — W — medium
- Kondrak & Hauer, ACL Aug 2017 (not 2018): AI said likely Hebrew alphagrams; experts unconvinced — W — disputed
- Nicholas Gibbs 2017 (TLS): abbreviated Latin women's-health guide; Davis: "doesn't result in Latin that makes sense" — W — rejected
- Gerard Cheshire 2019: "calligraphic proto-Romance"; Univ of Bristol distanced itself; Davis: "aspirational" — W — rejected
- 2020 German Egyptologist claim not accepted — A — rejected
- Tucker/Talbert 2014 Nahuatl/New Spain; Hansen called it "pure speculation" — W — disputed
- Expert Rene Zandbergen: text "has NOT YET been solved" — H — high

## Hoax vs real language
- Gordon Rugg 2003 (not 2004): meaningless text can be generated with prefix/stem/suffix table + Cardan grille; but grille invented ~1550, after the book — W — high
- Pro-language evidence: Zipf's law, word-length structure (Amancio et al. 2013); against: unusually low h2 — W — medium
- Dating effectively rules out Voynich forging it (Duffy); Baresch letter found 1999 — W — high
- Garry Shaw (author of Cryptic, May 2025) leans hoax, hopes he is wrong — H — opinion

## Recent news
- Sept 2024: multispectral scans of 10 pages released; revealed details (incl. a Prague owner's attempt at decoding symbols) — W, A — high (2 sources)
- Michael A. Greshko, Naibbe cipher, Cryptologia, online 26 Nov 2025: card-game-based verbose cipher turns Latin/Italian into Voynich-like text matching several statistics; he says it is NOT how the book was made and does not prove meaning — A (Jan 2026), S, W — high (3 sources)
- 2026 study (per Wikipedia): book originally unbound "singulions" — W — low (single source)
- Zenodo preprint "Voynich f99r deciphered" (Italian anagrams): unreviewed — disputed, do not present as fact
- Greshko analogy: gap from natural languages like London to Sydney — A — medium

## Angle
- Core twist: it behaves like a real language (Zipf, ~10 bits entropy) yet at letter level is too repetitive for any known language; real 15th-century parchment so no modern forgery; yet 600+ years, WWII codebreakers and AI have all failed. A Nov 2025 cipher study shows a "meaningful but weird" explanation is possible, but unproven.
- Misconceptions: Roger Bacon wrote it (impossible, dates ~150 years later); it has been "cracked" (every headline claim collapsed); Voynich forged it (carbon dating rules out).
