# Acidity (acid reflux / GERD) - facts (claim - source - confidence)

- GERD affects about 20% of people in the United States - NIH/NIDDK, https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/acid-reflux-ger-gerd-adults/definition-facts - high
- Parietal cells in the stomach lining secrete hydrochloric acid using H+/K+ ATPase "proton pumps"; triggered by gastrin, histamine, acetylcholine (vagus nerve, cephalic phase: sight/smell of food) - NCBI StatPearls Physiology, Gastric Acid - high
- Stomach pH about 1.5-3.5; pepsin digests protein - NCBI StatPearls - high
- Stomach protected by mucus + bicarbonate layer and rapid epithelial renewal (surface cells replaced every few days) - NCBI - high
- Lower esophageal sphincter (LES) = muscle ring at the end of the esophagus; reflux mostly from transient LES relaxations, triggered by stomach distension after meals - NIH/NCBI - high
- Lying down removes gravity help; raised abdominal pressure and hiatal hernia favor reflux - NIDDK - high
- Esophageal squamous lining lacks thick mucus; acid + pepsin widen intercellular spaces, injure cells, cause inflammation (esophagitis) - NCBI - high
- Long-term reflux can change esophageal lining to intestinal-type cells (Barrett's esophagus) in some people - NIDDK - high
- Heartburn = acid activating esophageal pain nerves (acid-sensing receptors such as TRPV1); felt behind the breastbone; regurgitation gives sour taste; extra saliva (water brash) - NCBI - high
- Reflux reaching throat/airways can cause hoarseness, cough - NIDDK - high
