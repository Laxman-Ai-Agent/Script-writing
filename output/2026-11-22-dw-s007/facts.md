# NASA Moon Base: up to $10B delivery contracts and PROMISE rover

## What it is
- CLPS-2 is a 10-year NASA contract with a maximum limit of $10 billion for private companies to deliver rovers and instruments to the Moon.
- The $10 billion is a ceiling, not money already paid out.
- Eight companies can compete: SpaceX, Blue Origin, Firefly Aerospace, Intuitive Machines, Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman, ispace-U.S., Voyager Technologies.

## How it works / how it happened
- Nov 7: NASA event at Kennedy Space Center where real orders were placed.
- VADR: rocket launch purchase contract; 6 orders totaling about $1.3 billion to Arrow Science and Technology, Blue Origin, Impulse Space.
- Missions to use Falcon 9, New Glenn and Terran R rockets.
- PROMISE rover: Blue Origin's Mark 1 lander takes it to the south pole; NASA JPL builds it; launch planned late 2028.
- Rover built largely from reused parts: a Curiosity test body and spare parts set aside for Curiosity and Perseverance.
- Power from MMRTG, a small nuclear generator, instead of solar panels.
- PROMISE mission: about 18 months of roving, 1 m drilling, looking for good base sites and ice.
- March 2026: NASA announced Moon Base plan in three phases.

## Why
- South pole craters never get sunlight; scientists think water ice may be hidden there.
- Water could provide drinking water, oxygen and rocket fuel.

## Twist / surprising facts
- Private lander record so far: Astrobotic Peregrine never reached the Moon; Intuitive Machines Odysseus landed but tipped over; Athena landed tipped; Firefly Blue Ghost 1 is the only full success.
- Isaacman: goal is "not flags and footprints... but staying there."
- Phase one and two (first 7 years) estimated around $20 billion.
- Some reports: with phase three, total could reach about $30 billion; phase three could run to 2036 or later.
