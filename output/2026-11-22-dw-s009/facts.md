# Where did humans originate? Why Africa

## What it is
- Science says all humans' roots are in Africa; the question is where and how humans began there.
- Humans and chimpanzees split from one common ancestor about 6-7 million years ago, in Africa; humans did not come from chimps.

## How it works / how it happened
- East African Rift: land layers moved apart over millions of years, creating valleys and mountains.
- Mountains changed rain patterns; dense forest broke into patches with grassland between.
- Walking on two legs may have developed because trees were far apart (hypothesis).
- Sahelanthropus skull (Chad, 2001, about 7 million years old): hole for spinal cord underneath, suggesting upright walking (not fully agreed).
- Laetoli, Tanzania: footprints about 3.6 million years old in volcanic ash show upright walking.
- Lucy skeleton (Ethiopia, found 1974), about 3.2 million years old, walked on two legs; shorter than modern humans.
- Dating: volcanic ash layers and lake sediments in the Rift; fossils dated from these layers.
- Stone tools: Lomekwi, Kenya, about 3.3 million years ago; better tools in Ethiopia about 2.6 million years ago.
- Jebel Irhoud, Morocco (found 2017): about 300,000 years old, modern-looking face, older brain shape.
- Out of Africa: a small group left Africa about 70,000-50,000 years ago; most people outside Africa descend from it (genetics).
- Early exits (Misliya, Israel; Apidima, Greece) did not last.
- Fire: some signs about 1 million years ago; regular use much later.

## Why
- Africa's varied environments (forest to desert) let early humans adapt and spread.
- Green Sahara periods opened routes out of Africa many times.
- Homo erectus left Africa earlier than sapiens: Dmanisi, Georgia, fossils about 1.8 million years old.

## Twist / surprising facts
- Paranthropus boisei and robustus, relatives, died out about 1 million years ago; cause still not certain.
- Swartkrans cave (South Africa): child skull with two holes 33 mm apart matching a leopard's lower canines; skull belongs to Paranthropus robustus.
- Shangchen, China: stone tools said to be 2.1 million years old; no bones found; age doubted by some.
- Outside-Africa people carry about 2% Neanderthal DNA.
- Australia: about 65,000 years ago per Madjedbebe tools; some question this.
- Americas: White Sands, New Mexico footprints about 21,000-23,000 years old.
- Egypt cave paintings of swimmers: 6,000-9,000 years old, far younger than the outward migration.
