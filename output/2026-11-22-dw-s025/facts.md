# Cyclone Akwan: third storm in three weeks toward Andhra coast

## What it is
- Cyclone Akwan is forming in the Bay of Bengal and moving toward the Andhra Pradesh coast.
- It is the third storm to hit the same coast in about three weeks.
- Name given by Iran; names come from a list of 13 countries in set order; the name has no link to storm strength.

## How it works / how it happened
- Previous storms: Arnab (Oct 28); Murasu (landfall near Ongole, Nov 3).
- Akwan: circulation over South Andaman Sea became low pressure, then a depression on Nov 15.
- Depression stage: wind 31-61 km/h.
- IMD forecast: cyclonic storm on Nov 16, severe cyclonic storm on Nov 17, possibly very severe later.
- Nov 16 position: about 960 km from Visakhapatnam, 800 km from Chennai; moving north-north-west at 15 km/h.
- Formation: sea above 26.5 degrees C; warm moist air rises, low pressure forms, winds rush in and spin due to Earth's rotation.
- Landfall expected Nov 20 between Nellore and Visakhapatnam (Andhra, Yanam coast).
- Landfall point changed several times: first Machilipatnam, then Bapatla, now Nellore to Visakhapatnam.

## Why
- Bay of Bengal stayed warm after the monsoon; atmosphere favors storms, so season is more active than usual (experts).
- Akwan is still a warm-sea-fueled storm; it keeps strengthening as long as warm water feeds it.

## Twist / surprising facts
- Murasu damage estimate for Andhra alone: Rs 2,484 crore.
- Akwan name comes from Shahnameh (Persian book): a demon called Akvan.
- Wind at landfall: 110-130 km/h in different forecasts.
- Red alert: Kakinada, Konaseema, East Godavari, West Godavari, Eluru, Krishna, Bapatla.
- Orange alert: Visakhapatnam and Vizianagaram on Nov 20; heavy rain in Yanam.
- Rain alerts in about 10 states, including Odisha, Tamil Nadu and Telangana.
- Sea rough until about Nov 20; fishermen cannot work.
