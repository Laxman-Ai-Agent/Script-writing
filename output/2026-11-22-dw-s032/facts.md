# GTA concept: cinema, freedom, satire, living world

## What it is
- GTA's core concept, per the script, rests on four pillars: interactive cinema, sandbox freedom, dark satire, living world.
- Founders: Sam Houser and Dan Houser, with Rockstar Games (owned by Take-Two).
- Origin: a police chase game (Race'n'Chase) by DMA Design in 1997; the team flipped the player into a criminal.

## How it works / how it happened
- Race'n'Chase tested as boring; team asked what if the player were the thief; this became Grand Theft Auto.
- Rockstar Games founded the year after (1998) by Sam and Dan Houser; DMA Design became Rockstar North.
- Pillar 1 (interactive cinema): camera angles, cutscenes and music borrowed from film; player controls story.
- Pillar 2 (sandbox freedom): player can skip missions, drive, use boats; crimes raise wanted level and police response.
- Pillar 3 (dark satire): cities mirror US society; radio ads, TV, politicians mocked.
- Pillar 4 (living world): NPCs, weather and relationships make the city feel alive even when unobserved.

## Why
- Dan Houser: GTA's success comes from freedom and combining shooting, driving and simulation games.
- Houser said GTA cities let players see America through movies and advertising.
- GTA 6 aims satire at social media; the first trailer looks like phone-shot viral clips and Reels.

## Twist / surprising facts
- GTA was first planned as a police game; criminals were the target only after testing.
- Freedom has consequences: the bigger the crime, the more police follow.
- GTA 6 story centers on trust, love and danger between Jason and Lucia.
- First trailer: over 90 million views in 24 hours, a viral record.
- Rockstar's Bengaluru team reportedly has many developers on GTA 6 (not officially confirmed).
