# Voyager 1 and Voyager 2: NASA's 49-year journey

## What it is
- Two NASA probes: Voyager 2 launched August 20, 1977; Voyager 1 launched September 5, 1977.
- Voyager 1 is now about 25,900 crore km (25.9 billion km) away, about one light-day from Earth.
- It is the farthest human-made object; reached interstellar space.

## How it works / how it happened
- Voyager 1 was planned to go faster, so it reached Jupiter first; hence the numbers (2 launched first).
- Grand Tour plan (Jupiter to Neptune, once every ~175 years alignment) was cut in 1972 to Jupiter and Saturn; Voyager 2 kept a later option.
- Voyager 1 at Jupiter (March 1979): volcanoes on Io seen, first volcanoes seen erupting off Earth.
- Voyager 2 at Jupiter (July 1979): cracked ice surface of Europa.
- Voyager 1 at Saturn (November 1980): thick atmosphere around Titan.
- Voyager 2 passed Saturn August 1981.
- Voyager 2 at Uranus (January 1986): 11 new moons, 2 new rings.
- Voyager 2 at Neptune (August 1989): new rings, 6 new moons, Great Dark Spot (later vanished), nitrogen geysers on Triton.
- Pale Blue Dot photo (February 14, 1990): Voyager 1 turned back for a family photo of the solar system; Earth a dot.
- Heliopause crossing: Voyager 1 on August 25, 2012; Voyager 2 in November 2018.

## Why
- Each probe carries a Golden Record (sounds, music, greetings, images of Earth) in case other life finds it.
- Grand Tour plan was rare: outer planets aligned once every ~175 years.

## Twist / surprising facts
- Radio signal takes about 24 hours one way from Earth to Voyager 1; a reply takes 2 days round trip.
- Launch power about 470 W from plutonium RTG, dropping about 4 W per year.
- Originally 10 instruments per probe; now only some work.
- Instruments turned off: Voyager 2 Low-energy Charged Particles (March 2025); Voyager 1 same instrument (April 2026).
- November 2023: Voyager 1 sent garbled data; a faulty memory chip was found; code moved to other memory (April 2024).
- Thrusters unused since 1980 were reactivated in 2017.
- Voyager 1 speed about 17 km per second.
- Oort Cloud, at the solar system's edge, is estimated about 300 years away (estimate).
- Engineering data may last until about 2036 at current power decline (estimate).
