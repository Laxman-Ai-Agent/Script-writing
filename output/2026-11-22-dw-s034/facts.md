# How an LED TV works: remote, backlight, LCD panel, pixels

## What it is
- An "LED TV" is an LCD TV with LED backlight; the LEDs only provide white light from behind the screen.
- The picture is made by the LCD panel, which blocks or passes light per pixel.
- OLED TVs are different: each pixel lights itself.

## How it works / how it happened
- Remote sends infrared (invisible) light pulses about 38,000 times per second, encoding button codes.
- TV's front sensor reads the pulses; a chip inside decides which button was pressed.
- Backlight: blue LED with yellow coating gives white light (blue + yellow looks white to the eye).
- Edge-lit TVs: LEDs around the edges; a light guide spreads light across the screen.
- Full-array TVs: LEDs directly behind the screen; local dimming splits them into zones.
- Mini-LED TVs: thousands of small LEDs, controlled in hundreds to thousands of zones.
- LCD panel: two crossed polarizer filters with liquid crystal between; voltage twists the crystal to let light through.
- Each dot has a red, green or blue filter; each subpixel has a TFT transistor switch.
- Frame: processor decodes compressed video into frames; screen updates 60 or 120 times per second.

## Why
- Dark scenes in LCD still leak some light; OLED can show true black because pixels switch off.
- LCD panels control light; the backlight is shared, so local dimming was added to improve contrast.

## Twist / surprising facts
- Blue LED discovery earned three scientists the 2014 Physics Nobel Prize (per script).
- Older TVs used CCFL tube lamps for backlight; LEDs replaced them.
- 4K screen: 3,840 x 2,160 pixels, about 8.3 million pixels, about 24.9 million subpixel shutters.
- 8-bit: 256 levels per color, about 16.7 million colors; 10-bit HDR: about 1.07 billion colors.
- Motion: script says screen does not move; brain fills in motion between frames.
- Persistence of vision (common explanation) is rejected by script's scientists; brain fills in motion instead.
