# NASA Perseverance rover on Mars: Jezero Crater, Cheyava Falls, samples

## What it is
- NASA rover Perseverance; launched July 30, 2020 from Cape Canaveral on an Atlas V rocket; landed February 18, 2021 in Jezero Crater on Mars.
- Jezero Crater is about 45 km wide; was once a lake fed by a river that built a delta.
- Main aim: look for signs of ancient microbial life; study rocks and past climate; seal rock samples in tubes for later return to Earth.

## How it works / how it happened
- Carried MOXIE (makes oxygen from Mars air) and the Ingenuity helicopter.
- Ingenuity flew 72 times; mission ended January 2024; rover still working.
- Rover first explored the crater floor, then the old river delta, then climbed the crater rim (end of 2024).
- Krokodillen area: clay rocks older than the crater.
- Total distance by October (script date Oct 11): 45.22 km; beat the Opportunity rover record.
- Over 90% of driving done with AutoNav self-driving system.
- July 2024: Cheyava Falls rock found in Bright Angel area of old river valley; leopard-spot patterns.
- Sapphire Canyon sample taken from that rock; spots contain vivianite and greigite minerals.
- September 2025: research published in Nature; NASA called it a potential biosignature.
- Now at Lac de Charmes, west of the crater rim; heading toward Idubi hills and Rainbow Falls (about 100 m rock block).

## Why
- Minerals in the spots formed at low heat without signs of very high heat or acid, which could point to life (script's reasoning).
- Samples need Earth labs to give a final answer, so return is key.

## Twist / surprising facts
- Perseverance carried 43 tubes; 29 rock and soil samples collected so far (NASA October update).
- 10 tubes left on the ground at Three Forks (Dec 2022 - Jan 2023) for a future pickup.
- Mars Sample Return cost estimate rose to about $11 billion (script: 1,100 crore dollars).
- January 2026: US Congress cut almost all money for that plan (script).
- China is also developing its own sample return mission.
- Mission extension runs at least until 2028.
- Acting NASA administrator Sean Duffy said humans have never been this close to finding life on Mars (script).
