# Chernobyl: how a reactor accident changed a whole city

## What it is
- Reactor 4 at the Chernobyl Nuclear Power Plant (Ukraine) exploded on the night of April 26, 1986.
- Nearby Pripyat had about 45,000 people; no family lives there now.
- Plant is the reference point for a 2,800 km2 exclusion zone, later expanded to 4,300 km2.

## How it works / how it happened
- Reactor basics: uranium splits and makes heat; heat turns water to steam that spins a turbine; control rods act like brakes.
- The night's safety test: check whether a slowing turbine could still power pumps during a power loss. Automatic shutdown systems were switched off first.
- Power was held near 200 MW after falling to about 30 MW; rule requires at least 15 control rods; only 8 were in place.
- RBMK design: more steam means more power, a flaw that is strongest at low power.
- 1:23 am: test began; power rose as steam increased.
- 36 seconds later, the AZ-5 emergency button was pressed; investigation never fully established who pressed it or why.
- Control rod tips were graphite; first moments of insertion pushed out neutron-absorbing water, so power spiked instead of falling.
- Within seconds the reactor blew: a steam explosion, then a second blast; graphite fire followed.
- 14 firefighters reached the site within 5 minutes, unaware of the radiation.
- About 36 hours after the blast, Pripyat residents were evacuated (promised return in 3 days).

## Why
- The emergency button made the disaster worse because of the graphite-tip flaw and steam-driven power rise.
- The flaw had shown up earlier at Lithuania's Ignalina plant (1983); no restrictions were placed (script).
- Soviet government gave no clear public information at first.

## Twist / surprising facts
- Most people call it an atom bomb blast; script says it was a steam explosion.
- Iodine-131 half-life about 8 days; Caesium-137 about 30 years.
- Radiation spread over Belarus, Scandinavia and much of Europe.
- Sweden's Forsmark plant (over 1,000 km away) set off alarms about two days later.
- New Safe Confinement (2016): 110 m tall, 36,000 tonnes, built to cover the reactor.
- Wildlife (wild horses, wolves) seen on camera in the zone.
- 2022: Russian army dug trenches in the radioactive soil.
- February 2025: a drone made about a 6 m hole in the shield roof; Ukraine blamed Russia, Russia denied.
- IAEA (December) said the shield lost its ability to contain radiation; repair cost about 500 million euros, target 2030.
