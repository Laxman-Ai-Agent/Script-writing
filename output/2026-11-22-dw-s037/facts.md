# Voynich Manuscript: a 600-year-old book nobody can read

## What it is
- Undeciphered manuscript held at Yale University library (USA); written on vellum (calfskin).
- About 240 pages survive; at least 28 pages missing.
- About half the pages are plant drawings; none of the plants has been identified with certainty.
- Other sections: stars and zodiac signs, women bathing in water with tube-like pipes, a foldout page, and medicine or recipe-like lists.

## How it works / how it happened
- Text: over 170,000 characters and about 38,000 words; about 8,000 are different words.
- No word shorter than 2 or longer than 10 letters appears in practice.
- Handwriting expert Lisa Fagin Davis: five different people wrote it.
- Codebreakers in and after World War II failed; William Friedman could not read a line; his wife Elizebeth Friedman wrote in 1962 that every attempt would end in failure.
- 2009: University of Arizona carbon dated the vellum to 1404-1438, ruling out a modern forgery of the material.
- 1639: first certain record; Georg Baresch wrote to Athanasius Kircher for help.
- Jan Marek Marci's letter claimed Rudolf II paid 600 ducats and that Roger Bacon wrote it; Bacon died in 1294, about 150 years before the vellum was made.
- 1912: Wilfrid Voynich bought it from Jesuits in Rome (hence the name).
- 1969: H. P. Kraus donated it to Yale.
- Claims rejected by experts: 1921 hidden code (ink cracks); 2017 AI Hebrew claim; Latin women's health book; 2019 Romance language.
- 2003: Gordon Rugg showed a card-grille method can produce similar text; the card method dates to about 1550 (after the manuscript).
- 2024: ten pages scanned with special light; details seen; Prague owner's decoding notes found.
- November 2025: Michael Greshko made a card-game cipher turning Latin and Italian into Voynich-like text that matched many statistics; he said it does not prove the book was written this way.

## Why
- Word frequency follows Zipf's law (as in Telugu and English); letter patterns repeat more than in any real language.
- The text behaves like language but does not look like a full language.

## Twist / surprising facts
- Scientists count only about 8,000 different words in ~38,000.
- Each new "solved" headline has been rejected by experts.
- Rudolf II and Roger Bacon story is false on dates.
- Gordon Rugg's card method appeared after the book was written.
