# Antimatter: what happens when it touches matter

## What it is
- Antimatter is the opposite twin of matter: same mass, opposite charge.
- Electron (negative) has positron twin (positive); proton has antiproton (negative charge).
- Antihydrogen atom: an antiproton with a positron orbiting it.
- When matter and antimatter meet, both vanish into energy (annihilation).

## How it works / how it happened
- 1928: Paul Dirac's equation for the electron gave a solution with positive charge (positron).
- 1932: Carl Anderson saw positron tracks in cosmic-ray photos.
- 1955: antiproton found at Berkeley (America).
- 1995: first antihydrogen atoms made at CERN.
- Natural antimatter: potassium-40 in bananas emits a positron about every 75 minutes (script estimate).
- CERN Antimatter Factory: protons smashed into metal target; Antiproton Decelerator and ELENA slow the antiprotons.
- Storage: Penning trap (vacuum, magnetic and electric fields hold antiprotons in mid-air).
- 2011: ALPHA team held antiprotons for 1,000 seconds; now about 100 hours.
- 2023: ALPHA-g showed antimatter falls down like normal matter (no anti-gravity).
- March (this year, per script): BASE-STEP team drove a portable trap with 92 antiprotons around the CERN campus for about 30 minutes.

## Why
- Antimatter is hard to keep because it touches walls and vanishes.
- Its energy is high because E = mc^2 converts almost all mass to energy.
- Matter and antimatter should have been made equally in the Big Bang, but the universe kept about one extra matter particle per billion pairs.

## Twist / surprising facts
- About 50 times more efficient than fusion in the Sun (script).
- 1 gram antimatter + 1 gram matter ~ 43 kilotons TNT; Hiroshima bomb ~15 kilotons (about one third).
- CERN makes about 40 crore (400 million) antiprotons per hour.
- A whole year's CERN antiprotons, converted to energy, would light a 100 W bulb for about 5 seconds (~500 J).
- Making 1 gram would cost trillions of dollars (long-held estimate).
- Hospitals already use positrons: PET scans.
- Antiproton cancer research done at CERN; antimatter rockets remain on paper.
