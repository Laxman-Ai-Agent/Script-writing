# Deepest ocean: Challenger Deep and life in the trench

## What it is
- Challenger Deep, at the south end of the Mariana Trench in the Pacific, is the deepest known point on Earth, about 11 km down.
- Average ocean depth is about 3,700 m; Challenger Deep is roughly three times deeper.
- Name comes from HMS Challenger, whose crew first measured the trench in 1875.

## How it works / how it happened
- Pressure at the bottom is about 1,070 times surface pressure (about one tonne per square cm on the body, per script).
- 1960, January 23: Don Walsh and Jacques Piccard reached the bottom in the Trieste bathyscaphe.
- No one went back for 52 years.
- 2012: James Cameron dove alone in Deepsea Challenger.
- 2019: Victor Vescovo dove in the Limiting Factor submarine to about 10,928 m (record for a human dive).
- 2020: Kathryn Sullivan (first American woman to walk in space) dived to the bottom.
- 2020: China's Fendouzhe submarine reached the bottom with three crew.
- By 2022, about 27 people had visited the bottom (script).

## Why
- Life at the bottom: amphipods (shrimp-like creatures such as Hirondellea gigas) survive there.
- Fish limit: script says fish body chemistry cannot handle pressure below about 8,200-8,400 m, so no fish seen at Challenger Deep.

## Twist / surprising facts
- Fish named Mariana snailfish (2017), caught at about 7,966 m.
- 2023: snailfish filmed at 8,336 m in Japan's Izu-Ogasawara Trench, deepest filmed fish.
- Pressure does not automatically crush life: animals there are adapted; only human-built spaces need thick metal.
- 2019 study: microplastic found in the stomach of every animal caught below about 10,890 m.
- 2017 study: amphipods had very high PCB industrial chemicals.
- JAMSTEC dive in 1998: plastic bag seen at about 10,898 m.
- Vescovo also reported a plastic wrapper on the bottom.
- Seabed 2030 project: in 2017 about 6% of seabed mapped to modern standard; now about 27-29%; target: full map by 2030.
