# Human brain: how it tricks us without our knowing

## What it is
- Brain is about 2% of body weight but uses about 20% of resting energy (script).
- About 86 billion neurons (script: 8,600 crore); each neuron has about 7,000 connections on average; total over 100 trillion connections (estimate).
- Neurons send electrical signals that are on/off like switches; signals can reach about 120 m per second in some nerves.

## How it works / how it happened
- A thought or memory is a pattern of many neurons firing together (like a stadium crowd holding up cards).
- Blind spot: where the optic nerve leaves the eye there are no light cells; brain fills the gap using surrounding colour and the other eye's input.
- Attention: 1999 Simons and Chabris basketball test; about half the viewers missed a gorilla walking through.
- Memory: each recall rebuilds the memory and parts can change.
- 1995: Elizabeth Loftus study with 24 people; a false memory of being lost in a shopping mall was adopted by about one in four (script says roughly 1 in 4).
- 2005: Johansson and Hall "choice blindness" test: photos secretly swapped; most people did not notice and gave reasons for the chosen face; 84% had said they would notice (script).
- Dreams: theories include memory processing and threat rehearsal; no agreed answer.
- 2012 mouse study: light reactivated a fear memory (script).

## Why
- The brain uses shortcuts: it fills gaps, drops unneeded details and rebuilds memory to save energy.
- Script's summary: we see a "best guess" of the world, not the world itself.

## Twist / surprising facts
- The "we use only 10% of the brain" idea is false; almost all parts do something.
- Gorilla test: many people who were looking at the passes still missed the gorilla.
- People invent reasons for choices they did not make.
- 1995 Loftus finding: memories can be changed from outside; the exact number was later debated.
- David Chalmers (1995) named consciousness the "hard problem"; no full answer yet.
