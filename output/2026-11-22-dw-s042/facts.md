# Nuclear submarines: months underwater and how they make air and water

## What it is
- Nuclear submarines are powered by a nuclear reactor; the real limit is the crew's food, not fuel.
- Most major navies' subs use Pressurized Water Reactors (America, Russia, Britain, France, China, India, per script).
- Normal patrol: 70-90 days; about 155 crew per Ohio-class submarine.

## How it works / how it happened
- 1955: USS Nautilus, the first nuclear submarine, sent the message "Underway on nuclear power" on leaving port.
- Three years later, Nautilus traveled under Arctic ice and reached the North Pole underwater.
- Reactor heats water; steam turns turbines, which turn the propeller shaft and generators.
- Ohio class: one reactor, two turbines, about 35,000 shaft horsepower on one shaft.
- Pressurized Water Reactor: water kept under high pressure; Boiling Water Reactors are louder and their water is radioactive, so unsuited for hiding.
- Fuel: many reactors' uranium core lasts 30-40 years; Columbia class core lasts the whole submarine life.
- Air: seawater is desalinated (distillation or reverse osmosis), then electrolysis splits water into hydrogen and oxygen; without desalting, chlorine gas forms.
- CO2 removed by scrubbers; backup oxygen candles (sodium chlorate and iron powder).
- Drinking water comes from desalinated seawater.
- Ohio class uses Blue and Gold crews: one team at sea, one ashore.

## Why
- A reactor provides the power to make oxygen and water, so the sub can stay submerged for months.
- Pressurized water reactors avoid the noise and radioactive water of boiling reactors.

## Twist / surprising facts
- USS Pennsylvania, January to June 2014: 140 days (about 5 months); record longest patrol for Ohio class.
- Ohio-class publicly stated test depth about 240 m (roughly an 80-storey building).
- Russian Typhoon and Borei estimated about 400 m; Virginia class claimed about 490 m by some, not confirmed.
- Russia's Typhoon: about 48,000 tons submerged, 175 m long, two reactors, up to six months underwater (per script).
- Russia's new Borei class carries 16 Bulava missiles.
- Columbia class: about $9 billion each, 16 Trident missiles, expected around 2031.
- 2019 counts: America 70, Russia 40, China 19, India 3 nuclear submarines.
- Reports say America uses about 93% enriched uranium for Columbia class; exact figures kept secret.
