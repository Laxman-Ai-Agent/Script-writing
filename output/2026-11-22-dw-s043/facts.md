# Lost city of Atlantis: legend, Plato, and real disasters

## What it is
- Atlantis is a legendary city/island said to have sunk in one day and night.
- Its first known account is in two works by Plato: Timaeus and Critias (around 360 BC, per script).
- Critias was unfinished; the story stops mid-way.

## How it works / how it happened
- Plato's account: Critias tells a story from Solon, an Athenian leader, who heard it from priests at Sais in Egypt.
- Island was beyond the Pillars of Heracles (Strait of Gibraltar), 9,000 years before Solon; bigger than Libya and Asia combined.
- Had a temple to Poseidon, canals in rings, and a large navy.
- Atlantis attacked Europe and Athens; Athens defeated them.
- Then earthquakes and floods sank the island in one day and night.
- No machines, aliens or advanced technology appear in Plato's text; it is a story of pride and punishment.

## Why
- Scholars say Plato invented Atlantis as a moral lesson on how a perfect state is ruined by arrogance.
- Translator Jowett thought the Egyptian priests and Solon frame was a device to make the story sound true.
- Possible real inspirations: Persian Wars memory, Santorini eruption, and Helike sinking (per script's summary).

## Twist / surprising facts
- Ignatius Donnelly (American politician) wrote an 1882 book arguing Plato's story was literally true; it started the Atlantis craze.
- Bimini Road (Bahamas): underwater stone rows are natural beachrock, which breaks into slabs.
- Eye of the Sahara (Mauritania): a rock dome eroding for about 100 million years, in a desert, not the sea.
- 2011 documentary claimed Atlantis near Cadiz (Spain); mainstream scientists rejected it; the tsunami remains there are about 2,400 years old, far from Plato's era.
- Santorini eruption about 1600 BC: 1,200 years before Plato, and Santorini is a small Aegean island, not a continent.
- Helike (Greece), 373 BC: a winter night earthquake and waves sank the city; land dropped 4-9 m; Plato was still alive.
- Helike Project (2001) found the city's remains.
