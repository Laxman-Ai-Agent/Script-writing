# Diabetes (blood sugar)

## Entry/trigger
- Carbohydrates in food are digested into glucose, which enters the bloodstream.
- Refined carbs (white rice, maida, cool drinks, fruit juice) digest fast and cause sharp glucose spikes.
- Fat accumulating around the abdomen, liver and muscles drives insulin resistance.
- Stress hormones such as cortisol push blood sugar up.
- Poor sleep, smoking, prolonged sitting and late heavy meals are listed as sugar-raising habits.
- Indians can develop type 2 diabetes at lower body weight and younger age, linked to abdominal and liver fat.

## Cellular chain
- Glucose (the cells' fuel) -> bloodstream -> must enter cells.
- Pancreas releases insulin.
- Insulin acts on muscle, liver and fat cells, opening entry for glucose; blood sugar falls.
- Type 2: insulin is still produced but cells stop responding (insulin resistance).
- Pancreas makes more insulin to compensate; over years it tires and glucose stays in the blood.
- Muscle contraction (walking, lifting) brings GLUT4 transporters to the cell surface, pulling glucose in without insulin.

## Immune/organ impact
- Sustained high blood sugar damages the eyes, kidneys and nerves.
- Liver and muscle fat accumulation is named as the site of insulin resistance.

## Symptom science
- Hypoglycaemia (sweating, tremor, dizziness) occurs when blood sugar drops too low, after insulin or sugar-lowering drugs combined with extra exercise or reduced food.

## Numbers & citations
- ICMR: over 100 million people in India have diabetes; about 130 million more are in the prediabetes range.
- DiRECT study (UK): 86% of participants who lost more than 15 kg reached normal blood sugar without medication (remission, not cure).
- UKPDS study: each 1% reduction in HbA1c was linked to about 37% lower risk of eye, kidney and nerve damage.
- HbA1c reflects average blood sugar over the past three months.
- Short post-meal walks of 10-15 minutes: "some small studies" (unnamed) report lower blood sugar; the effect lasts only 1-2 days.
