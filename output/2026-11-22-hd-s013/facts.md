# Diarrhea

## Entry/trigger
- Contaminated water and food introduce germs, the main cause.
- Viruses: rotavirus, norovirus. Bacteria: E. coli, Salmonella, Shigella. Parasite: Giardia.
- Germs spread between people through unwashed hands.
- Some germ toxins make the intestine release water instead of absorbing it (cholera pattern).
- Non-infectious triggers: antibiotics, lactose intolerance (undigested milk sugar), IBS.

## Cellular chain
- The intestine receives water from food, drink and digestive juices; the small intestine reabsorbs most of it, and the colon reabsorbs the rest ("sponge").
- A working sponge produces formed stool. Three or more loose, watery stools a day = diarrhoea.
- Toxin route: toxin makes the intestinal lining secrete water into the gut.
- Wall-damage route: germs damage the intestinal lining, causing blood in stool.
- Salt and sugar in the right proportion are absorbed by the small intestine and pull water along with them.

## Immune/organ impact
- The main danger is dehydration: loss of water and salts.
- Malnourished children have more frequent diarrhoea; each episode worsens the nutritional deficit (a cycle).

## Symptom science
- Thirst, dry mouth, low dark urine, dizziness and sunken eyes reflect fluid loss.
- Infants: no wet diaper for 3+ hours, no tears, sunken soft spot on the head, lethargy reflect fluid and salt loss.
- Blood in stool: germs damaging the intestinal wall.

## Numbers & citations
- NHS: most cases resolve in 5-7 days.
- NIH: untreated dehydration in children can cause death within a day.
- WHO: diarrhoea is the third leading cause of death in children under 5; about 444,000 child deaths per year.
- India study (unnamed): about 55,000 children under 5 died of diarrhoea in 2019.
- WHO: 10-14 days of zinc in children cut duration by about 25% and stool volume by about 30%.
- NHS: fruit juice and fizzy drinks can worsen diarrhoea.
