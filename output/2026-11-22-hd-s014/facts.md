# Thyroid disease

## Entry/trigger
- Hypothyroidism is most commonly caused by Hashimoto's disease: the immune system attacks the thyroid; more common in women and over-60s.
- Hyperthyroidism is most commonly caused by Graves' disease (immune-driven).
- Thyroid nodules, or excess thyroid hormone intake, can also cause hyperthyroidism.
- Iodine is needed to make thyroid hormones; iodine deficiency was historically a major cause of goitre. Iodine excess is also harmful.

## Cellular chain
- Thyroid gland (butterfly-shaped, front of neck) makes T3 and T4.
- Pituitary gland (brain) releases TSH to signal the thyroid.
- Low T3/T4 -> TSH rises to push more output; high T3/T4 -> TSH falls.
- High blood TSH indicates an underactive thyroid; low TSH indicates an overactive one.
- Thyroid hormones act as the body's accelerator: heart rate, body heat, speed of converting food to energy.

## Immune/organ impact
- Hashimoto's: immune attack on the thyroid. Graves': immune-driven hormone excess.
- Untreated hyperthyroidism can disrupt heart rhythm, raising stroke and heart failure risk, and can weaken bones.
- Low thyroid in pregnancy can affect both mother and baby.
- Rare emergencies: thyroid storm (hyperthyroid) and myxedema coma (severe hypothyroid).

## Symptom science
- Hypothyroid (slowed body): fatigue, weight gain, cold intolerance, dry skin, hair loss, constipation, low mood, heavy or irregular periods.
- Hyperthyroid (accelerated body): weight loss despite eating, palpitations, tremor, sweating, heat intolerance, insomnia, frequent urination.

## Numbers & citations
- Experts estimate over 40 million (4 crore) people in India have thyroid problems (organization unnamed).
- About 93% of Indian households use iodised salt (unnamed source).
- India's goitre control programme began in 1962 (unnamed source).
