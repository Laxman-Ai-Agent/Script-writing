# Epilepsy (seizure disorder)

## Entry/trigger
- A seizure is a sudden, uncontrolled electrical discharge in the brain.
- Epilepsy (WHO definition): two or more seizures without an identifiable trigger.
- Causes: brain injury at birth, head trauma, stroke, brain infections (meningitis), brain tumour, genetic conditions.
- Neurocysticercosis: pork tapeworm larvae lodge in brain tissue as cysts, after eggs are ingested via contaminated food or hands.
- About half of epilepsy cases have no known cause.
- Triggers: poor sleep, stress, alcohol, missed medication, flashing lights in some people.

## Cellular chain
- Normal brain: millions upon millions of nerve cells communicate through orderly, timed electrical signals.
- Seizure: an uncontrolled surge of electrical activity spreads through the brain.
- Neurocysticercosis: cysts form in brain tissue and trigger seizures.

## Immune/organ impact
- The brain is the affected organ; in neurocysticercosis the parasite lodges in brain tissue.
- Seizure-related falls, burns and drowning are injury risks.
- WHO: people with epilepsy have up to 3x higher risk of premature death; many of these deaths are preventable.

## Symptom science
- Tonic-clonic seizure: loss of consciousness, body stiffening, then rhythmic jerking.
- Absence seizure: seconds of staring and unresponsiveness; common in children and often mistaken for distraction.
- Some seizures begin with a single limb twitching, an unusual smell or an odd sensation.

## Numbers & citations
- WHO: about 50 million people worldwide have epilepsy.
- WHO: in low-income countries, about 3 in 4 people with epilepsy receive no treatment.
- WHO: with proper medication, about 70% can live seizure-free.
- WHO: about 25% of epilepsy cases are preventable.
- Indian studies (unnamed): people in rural areas rarely see a doctor after a seizure.
