# Asthma

## Entry/trigger
- Airways are hypersensitive: the immune system overreacts to harmless triggers such as dust or smoke.
- Risk factors: family history, allergies, eczema, obesity, preterm birth, childhood exposure to cigarette smoke, workplace dust and chemicals, air pollution.
- Attack triggers: dust mites, mould, pet hair, pollen, smoke, strong perfumes, cold air, exercise, colds, stress, weather change.
- Aspirin and ibuprofen can trigger attacks in some people.

## Cellular chain
- Air passes from the trachea into bronchi and smaller branches, lined by a soft layer and wrapped in thin muscle.
- During an attack, the airway lining becomes inflamed and swollen.
- Surrounding muscles tighten, narrowing the airway (bronchoconstriction).
- Excess sticky mucus further blocks airflow.
- Reliever inhaler: relaxes the tightened muscle; it does not reduce inflammation.
- Preventer (low-dose inhaled steroid): slowly reduces the underlying inflammation.

## Immune/organ impact
- Immune overreaction causing airway inflammation is the core mechanism.
- Long-term: airway walls can thicken.
- Severe attacks can require oxygen or nebuliser treatment in hospital.

## Symptom science
- Chest tightness and breathlessness: narrowed airway and tightened muscles.
- Wheezing: whistling sound as air passes through a narrowed passage.
- Difficulty breathing out: air is trapped by the narrowed passage.
- Night or early-morning cough: listed without a mechanism in the script.

## Numbers & citations
- WHO (2023): about 363 million people worldwide have asthma; about 442,000 deaths.
- WHO: most asthma deaths occur in low- and middle-income countries.
- Large Indian study (unnamed): about 37.9 million (3.79 crore) people had asthma in 2016.
