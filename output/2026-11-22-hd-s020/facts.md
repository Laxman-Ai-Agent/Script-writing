# Lung cancer

## Entry/trigger
- Long-term exposure to tobacco smoke damages DNA in airway-lining cells.
- WHO: tobacco accounts for 60-70% of preventable lung cancer cases.
- Non-smoker risks: outdoor air pollution, wood or dung cooking smoke, asbestos and silica dust, diesel exhaust, radon gas, family history.
- Second-hand smoke raises risk.

## Cellular chain
- Air travels through bronchi to alveoli, the site of oxygen exchange.
- Airway cells are constantly renewed; each cell's DNA sets when it should grow and when it should stop.
- DNA damage removes the stop signal; the cell divides uncontrollably, forming a tumour.
- Tumour cells spread through blood and lymph nodes to the brain, bones, liver and adrenal glands (stage IV).
- Non-small cell lung cancer is about 85% of cases; small cell is less common but grows faster.

## Immune/organ impact
- Spread sites named: brain, bones, liver, adrenal glands.
- Recurrent lung infections can appear as a symptom.

## Symptom science
- Early disease is often asymptomatic; early symptoms can resemble a regular cough.
- Persistent or worsening cough, coughing blood, chest pain, breathlessness, wheeze, hoarseness, unexplained weight loss and fatigue are listed without a mechanism in the script.
- A cough lasting more than three weeks is a warning sign.

## Numbers & citations
- WHO (2022): 2.5 million new lung cancer cases and 1.8 million deaths worldwide; the leading cause of cancer death.
- India (2022, unnamed): about 82,000 new cases and about 75,000 deaths.
- US figures (unnamed): 5-year survival is about 67% when confined to the lungs and about 12% after spread.
- Indian hospital study (unnamed): about 14% of patients were first given TB drugs, delaying diagnosis by about four months.
- Unnamed studies: the added risk roughly halves 10-15 years after quitting smoking.
