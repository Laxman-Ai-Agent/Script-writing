# Sleep apnea (obstructive)

## Entry/trigger
- During sleep, throat muscles relax; in some people the airway narrows or fully collapses.
- Risk factors: excess weight (especially neck fat), age, family history, large tonsils, smoking, sleeping on the back, alcohol before bed, sleep medications.
- Central sleep apnea: the throat is not blocked; the brain fails to send the breathing signal.
- AIIMS Delhi study: 13% of men and 5% of women have sleep apnea.

## Cellular chain
- Air passes from nose or mouth through the throat into the lungs.
- A narrowed airway: air squeezes through and the tissue vibrates, producing snoring.
- Complete blockage: blood oxygen falls; the brain senses this and briefly wakes the person.
- The arousal tightens throat muscles, reopening the airway.
- The cycle repeats throughout the night; these brief arousals are usually not remembered.

## Immune/organ impact
- Repeated oxygen drops and night-time alarms raise pressure on the heart and blood vessels.
- Linked to high blood pressure, heart disease, atrial fibrillation (irregular heartbeat), stroke and type 2 diabetes.
- Review (unnamed): severe untreated sleep apnea carries about 2.5x the stroke risk.

## Symptom science
- Loud snoring: vibration of the narrowed airway.
- Gasping or choking: the brain wakes the person to restore airflow.
- Morning headache, daytime sleepiness, poor concentration and irritability: attributed to repeated night-time awakenings.
- Frequent urination at night: listed without a mechanism.

## Numbers & citations
- AHI (events per hour): mild 5-14, moderate 15-29, severe 30 or more; each pause lasts at least 10 seconds.
- AIIMS Delhi estimate: about 100 million working-age Indians may have sleep apnea.
- Sleep study (polysomnography) records breathing, oxygen, heart rate and brain waves.
