# Malaria - facts (claim - source - confidence)

- 2023: about 263 million malaria cases, about 597,000 deaths worldwide - WHO World Malaria Report 2024, https://www.who.int/teams/global-malaria-programme/reports/world-malaria-report-2024 - high
- Spread only by bites of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes - WHO fact sheet, https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/malaria - high
- Mosquito injects sporozoites with saliva; they reach the liver and infect hepatocytes - CDC Biology of Malaria, https://www.cdc.gov/malaria/about/biology.html - high
- Sporozoite surface protein (CSP) binds liver-cell surface molecules (heparan sulfate proteoglycans) - NIH/NCBI review - high
- Liver stage is symptom-free; lasts roughly 1-2 weeks; one hepatocyte releases tens of thousands of merozoites - CDC / NCBI - high
- P. vivax / P. ovale leave dormant hypnozoites in the liver that cause relapses weeks to months later - CDC, NIH - high
- Merozoites invade red blood cells; parasite digests hemoglobin; toxic free heme is crystallized into hemozoin (malaria pigment) - NIH/NCBI - high
- Blood-stage cycle about 48 h (P. falciparum, vivax, ovale), 72 h for P. malariae; each schizont releases about 16-32 merozoites - CDC - high
- RBC rupture releases hemozoin and parasite GPI; macrophages release TNF-alpha, IL-1, IL-6 -> fever paroxysm - NIH/NCBI - high
- Cytokines raise the hypothalamic set point -> shivering + vasoconstriction (chills), then fever, then sweating when set point falls - standard physiology (NCBI StatPearls Fever) - high
- P. falciparum puts PfEMP1 "knobs" on infected RBCs -> they stick to small vessel walls (sequestration) -> cerebral malaria, kidney/lung injury - WHO, NIH - high
- RBC destruction -> anemia -> fatigue; spleen enlarges while clearing infected RBCs - CDC - high
